To get more furnace repair calls in winter, HVAC companies need to **show up the moment a homeowner searches "furnace not working" or "heating repair near me", look trustworthy at a glance, and answer the phone every time.** In practice that means an accurate Google Business Profile with winter hours, a dedicated furnace repair page that works on a phone, steady fresh reviews, ads scheduled for when you can really pick up, and a plan for after-hours calls.

Most of these jobs go to whoever is visible and reachable first. A cold house is an urgent problem, so people rarely compare ten companies. They call the first one or two that look reliable. The steps below focus on being one of those first two, starting with fixes you can make this week.

## Why winter calls are won differently

Summer AC calls and winter heating calls look similar, but winter searches tend to be more urgent and more local. When the heat goes out, people search on their phone, often at night or early morning, and they want someone today.

| What the customer is thinking | What they search | What wins the call |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "My heat stopped working" | "furnace not working", "no heat", "heating repair near me" | Map listing, reviews, a phone that's answered |
| "It's making a weird noise" | "furnace making loud noise", "furnace keeps turning off" | A helpful repair page with clear next steps |
| "Before it gets colder" | "furnace tune-up", "boiler service [town]" | Maintenance page, booking form |
| "This thing is old" | "new furnace cost", "furnace vs heat pump" | Replacement page, financing info |

Use your customers' words. In the US and Canada it's "furnace" and "heat". In the UK it's "boiler" and "central heating". In Australia and New Zealand, winter falls from June to August, and people search for "heater repair", "gas heater service" or "heat pump" (which is what many New Zealanders call a split system). The steps are the same. Only the words and the timing change.

## Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile ready for winter

For "near me" searches, the map results usually get attention before anything else. Our guide on [how to rank higher on Google Maps](/blog/how-to-rank-higher-on-google-maps/) covers the full picture. For winter, these are the parts that matter most:

- **Hours that match reality.** Google's guidelines ask for your regular customer-facing hours, and say you may use your current seasonal hours as your regular hours ([Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). If you take emergency calls in the evening or on weekends, and someone really answers, show it. Don't list hours you can't cover.
- **Holiday hours.** Use **Special hours** for Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year and other holidays. Google suggests confirming holiday hours even when they're the same as usual, so customers know they're accurate ([how to set special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076)). A profile with an "hours might differ" warning on Christmas Eve can lose calls to one with confirmed hours.
- **Services.** Make sure "Furnace repair", "Furnace installation", "Boiler repair", "Heat pump repair" and "Heating maintenance" are listed, if you do them.
- **Business name.** Keep it your real name. Google's guidelines don't allow extras like "Open 24 hours" in the name ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)), and keyword stuffing can get a profile suspended.
- **Photos and posts.** Add a few recent photos of real furnace and boiler jobs, and a post about your winter availability or tune-up booking.

## Step 2: Build a furnace repair page that converts on a phone

A single "Heating and Cooling" page rarely ranks well for "furnace repair" in your town, and it doesn't reassure someone whose house is cold. Create a dedicated page for heating repair (or one per system, if you do furnaces, boilers and heat pumps in volume).

Put these near the top:

- **A tap-to-call phone number** and the words "Furnace repair in [your town and area]"
- **When you can come out:** same day, next day, evenings or 24/7, only if true
- **The areas you cover**, named clearly
- **A short "before you call" check:** thermostat set to heat, batteries, breaker, furnace filter. It builds trust, and people who've tried the easy fixes still call.
- **Safety first:** tell people that if they smell gas, they should leave the house and call their gas company or emergency services from outside. If their carbon monoxide alarm goes off, they should get out and call for help. It's the right thing to say, and it shows you know your trade.

Further down, cover common problems you fix, brands you service, what affects the cost, warranty or guarantee details, and a few FAQs. If you mention prices, use real ranges from your own jobs or a call-out fee you actually charge. Don't copy numbers from elsewhere.

Then check the page loads quickly and reads well on a phone. Our [free website audit](/tools/website-audit/) flags slow pages and missing basics, and the [Google search preview tool](/tools/google-search-preview/) lets you test a title like "Furnace Repair in Columbus, Same-Day Service | Smith Heating" before you publish it.

**A tip from client work:** when we look at HVAC websites in late fall, the most common gap is that the homepage and main menu still lead with AC. The heating repair page exists, but it's buried three clicks deep. Simply adding "Furnace Repair" to the main menu and a winter banner with the phone number at the top of the homepage is a small change that helps both Google and customers find the right page.

## Step 3: Get fresh reviews that mention heating

When people compare two companies on the map, reviews are often the deciding factor, and recent reviews carry weight with customers because they show you're active now.

- **Ask after every heating job,** especially emergency repairs, when customers are most relieved.
- **Make it one tap.** Send your review link by text before you leave the driveway. Our [review request generator](/tools/review-request-generator/) writes the message and makes a QR code for your invoices.
- **Let customers describe the job.** Reviews that naturally mention "furnace", "no heat" or the town help other customers trust you. Never write reviews for customers or offer anything in exchange for good ones.
- **Reply to every review**, including negative ones, calmly and briefly.

More ideas are in our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## Step 4: Run ads only when you can answer

Paid ads can bring heating calls fast during a cold snap, but only pay for calls you can actually take.

**Local Services Ads (pay per lead).** In the US, Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. HVAC is in the first group, starting in August 2026, and the ads still appear in the same places on Search and Maps ([Google's migration guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585)). Your ad schedule moves across with you. Whichever dashboard you use:

- **Set an ad schedule** that matches when your phones are covered. Google lets you run ads all day, only during business hours, or on custom hours ([ad scheduling help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9251526)).
- **Pause when you're fully booked.** Leads you can't serve waste money and annoy customers.
- **Check your budget before a forecast cold spell.** After migration, budgets are set daily rather than weekly, and spend can vary day to day within the monthly limit.
- **Keep your Business Profile accurate,** because after migration your name, address and hours sync from your profile to your ads.

**Search ads (pay per click).** These work well for winter searches too, but they need careful keyword and location settings. Read [are Google Local Services Ads worth it?](/blog/google-local-services-ads-worth-it/) before deciding where your budget goes.

## Step 5: Answer every call, day and night

This is where most winter calls are lost. A homeowner with no heat who reaches voicemail usually calls the next company on the list.

| Situation | What to have in place |
| --- | --- |
| During business hours | Someone always on the phone, not a tech on a roof |
| Evenings and weekends | An on-call rotation or an answering service that can book or escalate |
| Missed calls | A text back within minutes ("Sorry we missed you, we can help, reply here or call…") |
| Fully booked | Be honest about the next available slot, and offer a waitlist for cancellations |
| Web forms | Reply within the hour during the day, with a phone call rather than an email |

Track how many calls you miss. Many phone systems and call tracking tools show this. If you're missing a lot of calls in a cold snap, fixing that will usually bring in more jobs than any extra marketing.

## Step 6: Bring back the customers you already have

Your easiest winter calls come from people who already trust you.

- **Fall tune-up reminders:** email or text past customers before the first cold week, offering a heating check.
- **Maintenance plans:** customers on a plan call you first when something breaks. If you offer plans, give them their own page and mention them on every invoice.
- **Fridge magnets and stickers:** a sticker on the furnace with your number and a "last serviced" date still works.
- **A simple winter post** on your Business Profile and social pages: "Heating trouble? We're taking same-day calls in [area]."

## Step 7: Publish a few helpful winter guides

You don't need a busy blog. Two or three useful pages, published or refreshed before the cold arrives, can bring in searches that turn into calls:

- "Furnace not turning on: what to check before you call"
- "Why is my furnace blowing cold air?"
- "How often should a furnace (or boiler) be serviced?"

End each one with a clear next step: "Still not working? Call us." Our [HVAC SEO guide](/blog/hvac-seo/) explains how to plan seasonal content a month or two ahead of each peak.

## Common mistakes that cost winter calls

- Holiday hours left unconfirmed, so customers can't tell if you're open
- Homepage still promoting AC in December
- No dedicated furnace or heating repair page
- Ads running at night with nobody to answer
- Not asking for reviews during the busiest weeks, when you do the most jobs
- Listing "24/7" when after-hours calls go to voicemail

## Frequently asked questions

### When should HVAC companies start winter marketing?

Ideally a month or two before the first cold spell, so Google has time to pick up page changes and you can refresh your profile and reviews. If winter has already started, focus on the quick wins first: hours, holiday hours, the phone number at the top of your site, and answering every call.

### Should I list my business as open 24 hours?

Only if someone really answers and can help at any hour. Google asks for your real customer-facing hours. If you offer emergency call-outs but not a full 24-hour service, say so clearly on your website and set your profile hours to what you can cover.

### Are Local Services Ads worth it for furnace repair?

For many HVAC companies they can be, because emergency heating jobs are valuable and you pay per lead rather than per click. They work best when you answer quickly and have strong reviews. Set an ad schedule that matches your phone cover.

### How do I get more heating calls without paying for ads?

Work on your Google Business Profile, a strong furnace repair page, fresh reviews and past customers. These take a little longer than ads but keep bringing calls every winter.

### Does this work for boilers and heat pumps too?

Yes. Use the same steps and the words your customers use: "boiler repair" in the UK, "heat pump repair" or "heater service" in Australia and New Zealand, and "furnace" in North America.
