Most small businesses don't need expensive SEO software. **Google's own free tools cover almost everything a local business needs**: Google Search Console for your website's search performance, Google Business Profile for Maps, PageSpeed Insights for speed, and Google Analytics for what visitors do. Add Bing Webmaster Tools and a couple of free helpers, and you're well equipped.

Here are the free tools worth using, what each one is for, and a simple routine to get value from them without spending hours.

## The essential free tools at a glance

| Tool | What it's for | Who makes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Search Console | Which searches show your site, indexing problems | Google |
| Google Business Profile | Your Maps listing, calls, reviews and insights | Google |
| PageSpeed Insights | Website speed and Core Web Vitals | Google |
| Google Analytics | What visitors do on your site | Google |
| Google Trends | How search interest changes over time | Google |
| Google Keyword Planner | Keyword ideas and rough search volumes | Google (inside Google Ads) |
| Bing Webmaster Tools | Your performance on Bing | Microsoft |
| Google search itself | Seeing what ranks, "People also ask" questions | Google |

## 1. Google Search Console

If you only set up one tool, make it this one. Search Console shows:

- **Which searches** your website appears for, and how often people click
- **Which pages** are indexed, and why others aren't
- **Problems** Google finds on your site
- **Links** from other websites

Our step-by-step [Google Search Console setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-search-console/) walks you through it.

**Best use for small businesses:** open the Performance report, sort by impressions, and look for searches where you appear on page two (average position 11 to 20). Improving those pages is often the quickest SEO win you'll get.

## 2. Google Business Profile insights

Your Business Profile shows how customers find and interact with your listing: searches, calls, direction requests and website clicks. For local businesses, this is often more important than website rankings.

**Best use:** check monthly which searches found your profile and whether calls are going up as you add reviews and photos.

## 3. PageSpeed Insights

[PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) tests how fast your pages load on mobile and desktop, and shows real-user Core Web Vitals data when your site has enough traffic.

**Best use:** test your homepage and top service pages on mobile. Focus on fixing the biggest issues first, usually oversized images. See our guide to [making your website faster](/blog/how-to-make-my-website-faster/).

## 4. Google Analytics

Google Analytics shows what visitors do once they're on your site: where they came from, which pages they view and whether they contact you.

**Best use:** set up tracking for your most important actions (form submissions, phone number taps) so you can see which traffic sources bring enquiries, not just visits. If Analytics feels overwhelming, focus on just these few reports.

## 5. Google Trends

[Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/) shows how interest in a search term changes over time and by region. It doesn't give exact volumes, but it's useful for:

- Comparing two ways of saying the same thing ("air conditioning repair" vs "AC repair")
- Spotting seasonal patterns, so you can publish content before demand peaks

## 6. Google Keyword Planner

Keyword Planner is inside Google Ads. You need a Google Ads account to use it, but you don't have to run ads. It gives keyword ideas and broad search volume ranges.

**Best use:** check which phrases people use for your services in your area before writing service pages.

## 7. Bing Webmaster Tools

Bing is a smaller search engine than Google, but it still sends traffic, and some other search experiences draw on Bing's results. [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters) is free and can import your site straight from Google Search Console.

## 8. Google search itself

Don't overlook the obvious. Searching for your own services shows you:

- **Who ranks** in the map results and website results, and what their pages look like
- **"People also ask" questions,** which are great ideas for FAQs and articles
- **Related searches** at the bottom of the page
- **Autocomplete suggestions** as you type, which reflect common searches

Search in a private or incognito window, and remember results vary by location.

## What about paid tools like Ahrefs and Semrush?

Paid SEO platforms are powerful for agencies and businesses competing nationally. Many offer free versions or limited free tools. For most local businesses, though, they're optional. The Google tools above, used consistently, will get you most of the way.

## A simple monthly SEO routine with free tools

Set aside an hour a month:

1. **Search Console:** check clicks and impressions, look for page-two searches, and check for indexing problems.
2. **Google Business Profile:** check calls and searches, reply to any unanswered reviews, add new photos.
3. **PageSpeed Insights:** re-test your homepage after any big website change.
4. **Analytics:** see which sources brought enquiries.
5. **Google search:** look at "People also ask" for one service, and add an answer to your site.

Write down three numbers each month: **website clicks, profile calls and enquiries.** If they trend up, your SEO is working.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free SEO tool for a small business?

Google Search Console. It's free, it comes straight from Google, and it shows the real searches people use to find your website, along with any indexing problems. For local businesses, pair it with your Google Business Profile performance data.

### Is Google Search Console the same as Google Analytics?

No. Search Console shows what happens **before** people reach your site: which searches you appear for, your average position and how many people click. Analytics shows what happens **after** they arrive: which pages they view and whether they contact you. They work best together.

### Can I do keyword research for free?

Yes. Start by listing the words customers actually use when they call or email you. Then use Google's autocomplete suggestions, "People also ask" boxes and related searches, Google Trends for comparing phrases, and Keyword Planner for rough volume ranges. Search Console will also show you the searches you already appear for, which is often the most useful list of all.

### Are free SEO audit tools accurate?

Free audit tools can be helpful for spotting obvious issues like missing page titles, broken links or slow pages. But they often flag minor issues as urgent and give generic scores. Use them as a checklist, and prioritize anything Search Console itself reports, because that comes from Google.

### How often should I check my SEO tools?

Once a month is plenty for most small businesses. Checking daily tends to cause worry over normal ups and downs. Look at trends over several months instead.

## Summary

- Google Search Console, Google Business Profile, PageSpeed Insights and Google Analytics cover most small business needs.
- Google Trends and Keyword Planner help with choosing words customers use.
- Bing Webmaster Tools is quick to add and free.
- Paid tools are optional for most local businesses.
- An hour a month with free tools beats an expensive subscription you never open.
