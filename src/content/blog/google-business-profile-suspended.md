If your Google Business Profile has been suspended, don't panic and **don't create a new profile.** Most suspensions happen because something on the profile appears to break Google's guidelines, and many can be reversed by fixing the problem and submitting an appeal with the right evidence.

This guide explains the common reasons for suspensions, how to fix them, and the exact appeal process Google describes, step by step.

## What a suspension means

Google says it may suspend or disable Business Profiles that don't follow its guidelines. When that happens, the profile may disappear from Google Maps and Search, and you may not be able to manage it ([Google's help page on suspended profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145)).

There's also a separate, lighter kind of action: **review restrictions.** If Google detects fake reviews, it can temporarily stop new reviews, unpublish existing ones, or show a warning on your profile ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287)). That's a different problem from a full suspension.

## Common reasons profiles get suspended

Google rarely tells you the exact trigger, but these are the usual suspects, based on Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177):

| Reason | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Extra words in the business name | "Ace Locksmith 24/7 Cheap Locksmith Sydney" |
| Ineligible address | Virtual office, PO box, mail drop, or an address where you don't actually operate |
| Address shown when it should be hidden | A home-based service business displaying its home address |
| Duplicate profiles | Two profiles for the same business at the same location |
| Big sudden edits | Changing name, address and category at once |
| Service area too big | Covering a whole state from one base |
| Phone or website issues | Call-center numbers or a website link that redirects elsewhere |
| Ineligible business type | Online-only businesses with no in-person contact |

Sometimes suspensions happen even when you've done nothing wrong, often after a legitimate change. The appeal process still applies.

## Step 1: Find out what Google has flagged

Log into your profile and read any notice carefully. Google's appeal tool shows the decision details, including the reason and the related policy where available.

## Step 2: Fix the problem before you appeal

Appealing without fixing the cause usually fails. Go through your profile against the table above:

- **Name:** change it to exactly what's on your signage, letterhead and registration documents.
- **Address:** make sure it's a real location where you operate. If customers don't visit, hide it and set a service area.
- **Categories:** keep them accurate and few.
- **Service area:** Google recommends no more than about two hours' driving time from your base.
- **Phone and website:** your own local number and your own site.

If you have duplicate profiles, deal with those too.

## Step 3: Gather your evidence

Google says you can upload evidence with your appeal. Examples it mentions include ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145)):

- Official business registration
- Business license
- Tax certificates
- Utility bills (electricity, phone, water, internet)

The business name and address on your documents should **match your profile exactly.** Photos can also help show you're real, such as your permanent signage, your premises or your branded vehicle, though Google doesn't list these as required.

## Step 4: Submit the appeal

According to Google's help page, the process is:

1. Go to the Business Profile appeals tool (linked from [Google's suspended profiles help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145)).
2. Sign in with the Google Account that manages the profile.
3. Select the profile, then the decision you want to appeal.
4. Review the decision details.
5. Upload your evidence and explain briefly why the profile should be reinstated.
6. Submit.

Keep your explanation short and factual: who you are, what you fixed, and what your documents prove.

If your **whole Google Account** has been restricted (not just the profile), Google says to appeal the account restriction first, then the profile.

## Step 5: Wait, and don't make changes

Google reviews appeals and emails you the decision. It doesn't give a fixed timeframe. While you wait:

- **Don't create a new profile** for the same business. Google explicitly says not to.
- **Don't keep editing** the suspended profile.
- **Don't submit repeated appeals.** Google says each appeal can be submitted only once.

## If your appeal is denied

Google says that if a reinstatement request is denied, you may be able to request one more review with **new** evidence through the local appeals contact form. Use this only when you have something new to show, such as an updated document or proof of a change you made.

## How to avoid future suspensions

- Use your real-world business name, nothing more
- Use a real address you operate from, or hide it if customers don't visit
- Never use virtual offices or PO boxes
- One profile per business location
- Make big changes one at a time, and only when necessary
- Keep your service area realistic
- Don't buy reviews or offer incentives for them
- Check your profile regularly for suggested edits from other users

## What to do while you're suspended

Losing your profile hurts, especially for local businesses that get most calls from Maps. In the meantime:

- Make sure your website clearly shows your phone number and location
- Make sure you're listed on Apple Maps, Bing Places and key directories
- Ask regular customers to save your number
- Consider ads if you need calls urgently

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a Google Business Profile appeal take?

Google doesn't publish a fixed timeframe. It reviews the appeal and emails you the decision, and the status shows in the appeals tool. Avoid editing the profile or sending duplicate appeals while you wait, since each appeal can only be submitted once.

### Will I lose my reviews if my profile is suspended?

While a profile is suspended, it may not be visible, so customers can't see your reviews. If the profile is reinstated, reviews generally come back with it. This is one of the main reasons not to create a new profile: you'd be starting again from zero, and Google says not to do it.

### Can a competitor get my profile suspended?

Competitors can suggest edits to your profile or report it. That's why it's worth checking your profile regularly for changes you didn't make, and keeping everything compliant so there's nothing legitimate to report. If incorrect edits appear, correct them and keep evidence of your real details.

### Why was my profile suspended after I changed my address?

Big edits, especially to name, address or category, can trigger a review. If the new details don't match what Google can verify, the profile may be suspended. Make major changes one at a time, make sure your signage and documents match, and be ready to re-verify.

### Should I hire someone to fix a suspension?

Many suspensions can be resolved yourself by fixing the cause and submitting a clear appeal with matching documents. If your situation is complicated, such as multiple locations or repeated suspensions, an experienced local SEO specialist may help. Be wary of anyone promising guaranteed reinstatement.

## Suspension recovery checklist

- [ ] Read the notice and identify the likely cause
- [ ] Fix the name, address, categories and service area
- [ ] Remove any duplicate profiles
- [ ] Gather registration, license, tax or utility documents matching your profile
- [ ] Submit one clear appeal through Google's appeals tool
- [ ] Don't create a new profile or keep editing
- [ ] Use the one extra review option only with new evidence
