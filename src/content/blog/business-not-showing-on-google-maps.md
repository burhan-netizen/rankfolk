If your business isn't showing up on Google Maps, the cause is usually one of a handful of things: **your profile isn't verified yet, it's been suspended, it's incomplete, or it exists but ranks too low for the search you tried.** Each one has a different fix.

Start by working out which situation you're in, then use the matching fix below. Most problems can be solved without paying anyone.

## First: is your business missing, or just not ranking?

These are two very different problems. Here's a quick test:

1. Open Google Maps and search for your **exact business name** plus your town.
2. Then search for your **service** plus your town (for example "dog groomer Wellington").

| Result | What it means | Jump to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You don't appear for your own name | Profile missing, unverified, suspended or hidden | Fixes 1 to 5 |
| You appear for your name, not your service | Profile exists but ranks low | Fixes 6 to 10 |

Search from a phone with location on, as well as a computer. Results change depending on where the searcher is.

## Fix 1: You don't have a profile, or someone else claimed it

Search for your business at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com). If you've never created a profile, follow our [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/).

If Google says the business is already managed by someone else (an old employee, a previous agency or a former owner), you can request access through the profile. Google will contact the current owner and give them time to respond.

## Fix 2: Your profile isn't verified

New profiles often don't appear publicly, or appear with limited information, until they're verified. Google says verification helps your business appear more often in results ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

Log in and check for a "Get verified" button. Google decides which verification method you can use: phone, text, email, video recording, live video call or postcard ([Google's verification help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)). Our guide to [verifying your Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-video-verification/) walks through each.

## Fix 3: Your profile is suspended or disabled

If your profile breaks Google's guidelines, Google can suspend or disable it, and it may vanish from Maps. Common triggers include:

- Keywords or locations added to the business name
- Using a virtual office, PO box or an address where you don't operate
- Duplicate profiles for the same business
- Sudden major changes to name, address or category

Log in and look for a notice. If it's suspended, read our guide on [what to do when your Google Business Profile is suspended](/blog/google-business-profile-suspended/). Don't create a new profile to get around it. Google specifically says not to.

## Fix 4: Your edits are still under review

After you change important details like your name, address or category, Google may review the edit. During that time your profile might show old details or appear inconsistently. Wait a few days, and avoid making lots of changes in a row.

## Fix 5: You're a service-area business with odd settings

If you hide your address (because customers don't visit you), your profile shows a service area instead of a pin. That's correct. But if your service areas are missing or set strangely, you may not show where you expect. Check that your service areas match where you actually work, within about two hours' drive of your base as Google recommends.

## Fix 6: The profile is incomplete

Google ranks on relevance, and an incomplete profile gives it little to match. Check:

- **Primary category:** is it the most specific option? "Italian restaurant" not "Restaurant", "Roofing contractor" not "Contractor".
- **Services and products:** are they listed?
- **Description:** does it plainly say what you do and where?
- **Hours:** are they accurate, including holiday hours?
- **Photos:** real, recent photos of your work and premises.

## Fix 7: You're too far from the searcher

Distance is one of Google's three local ranking factors, and it's the one you can't change. If you're on the edge of town, you'll naturally appear less for searches made on the other side.

What you can do is strengthen relevance and prominence (fixes 6, 8 and 9) so you compete better at a distance. Don't try to fake a closer address. It breaks the rules and risks suspension.

## Fix 8: You don't have enough reviews

Prominence includes review count and rating. If competitors have 150 reviews and you have 6, they will usually outrank you. Start asking every customer consistently. Our guide on getting more Google reviews has templates.

## Fix 9: Your website and other listings are weak

Google also looks at how well known you are across the web, including links to your website. Help it by:

- Linking your Google profile to your website, and your website back to your profile
- Making sure your name, address and phone number match on your website and directories
- Getting listed with local associations, suppliers and trusted directories

## Fix 10: You're searching for the wrong thing

Many owners search for a broad term like "lawyer" and panic. Try the specific searches your customers would use, such as "family lawyer Brisbane" or "will writing near me". You may already rank for those.

## How long until my business shows up?

- **After verification:** usually soon after, though it can take a few days to appear consistently.
- **After a suspension is lifted:** typically shortly after reinstatement.
- **After improving rankings:** weeks to months, depending on competition.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my business show on Google Maps for me but not for customers?

Google tailors local results to the searcher's location and the exact words they use. You may rank well near your base and lower across town. Ask a few customers in different areas what they see, and check the searches listed in your Google Business Profile performance report for a more accurate picture.

### Can I pay Google to show up higher on Maps?

No. Google states there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You can pay for ads, which appear separately and are labeled as sponsored, but they don't change where your free listing ranks.

### How do I know if my profile is suspended?

Log in to your Business Profile. If it's suspended or restricted, you'll usually see a notice explaining that the profile has been disabled or that it violates a policy, and Google typically emails the owner. Your profile may also disappear from Maps when people search your exact name.

### Does my website affect my Google Maps ranking?

Indirectly, yes. Google's prominence factor includes information from across the web, such as links to your business. A clear website with matching contact details, service pages and a link to your profile supports your listing.

### I moved premises. Why does my old address still show?

If you change address, update your profile, and expect the change to go through a review. Google's policies say that if a business has relocated, the old profile should generally be marked as permanently closed and a new one created at the new location, so check Google's current guidance for your situation before making changes.

## Checklist: business not showing on Google Maps

- [ ] Profile exists and you have access
- [ ] Profile verified
- [ ] No suspension or policy notice
- [ ] Real business name, no added keywords
- [ ] Specific primary category
- [ ] Address or service area correct
- [ ] Hours, services, description and photos complete
- [ ] Steady flow of reviews
- [ ] Website linked and details consistent everywhere
- [ ] Tested with realistic customer searches

Work through these in order. Most businesses find the problem in the first five checks.
