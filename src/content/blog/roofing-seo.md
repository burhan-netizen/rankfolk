**Roofing SEO** is the work of getting your roofing company to show up on Google when homeowners in your area search for roof repairs, replacements and inspections. Because a new roof is one of the biggest purchases a homeowner makes, a single job from Google can pay for months of SEO effort.

Roofing is also one of the most competitive local trades online, so the basics matter more, not less. This guide covers the steps that actually move roofers up the rankings: your Google Business Profile, your service pages, project proof, reviews and storm-season readiness.

## What homeowners search for

Roofing searches usually fall into four groups:

| Situation | Typical searches |
| --- | --- |
| Something is wrong | "roof leak repair near me", "missing shingles", "emergency roof tarp" |
| Planning a replacement | "roof replacement cost", "metal roof vs shingles", "best roofers in Perth" |
| After a storm | "storm damage roof repair", "hail damage roof inspection", "roof insurance claim help" |
| Specific services | "flat roof repair", "gutter replacement", "skylight installation" |

A good roofing website has a clear answer for each group.

## Step 1: Build a strong Google Business Profile

Your Google profile drives the map results, which is where many roofing calls start. Google ranks those results on relevance, distance and prominence ([Google's local ranking guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

For roofers specifically:

- **Primary category:** "Roofing contractor". Add secondary ones like "Gutter cleaning service" or "Skylight contractor" only if you really offer them.
- **Service-area business:** if customers don't come to your yard or office, hide the address and set your service areas.
- **Services:** list repairs, replacements, inspections, gutters, flat roofs, metal roofs and anything else you do.
- **Photos:** drone shots, before and after, crew at work (with safety gear on), finished roofs from the street.
- **Business name:** your real name only. Adding "Best Roofer Dallas" to it breaks Google's guidelines.

## Step 2: One page for every roofing service

Roofing customers search for specific jobs, so your website needs specific pages. A typical roofer might have:

- Roof repair
- Roof replacement
- Roof inspections
- Storm and hail damage repair
- Metal roofing
- Flat and commercial roofing
- Gutters and downpipes
- Skylights and roof ventilation

Each page should explain the service, show real photos of that type of job, answer common questions and offer a clear next step, usually a free inspection or quote.

If you serve a few distinct cities, a page for each can work, but only if it's genuinely useful: local projects, local photos, reviews from that area, any local building rules you deal with. Pages where only the city name changes rarely help.

## Step 3: Show proof, because roofing is a trust purchase

Homeowners worry about being ripped off by roofers, especially after storms. Your website should answer that worry before they ask:

- **Licenses and insurance** (where required in your area), with numbers they can check
- **Manufacturer certifications** and warranties you offer
- **A project gallery** organized by service, with short write-ups
- **Real reviews** on the page, ideally linked to your Google profile
- **Your team:** names and faces build trust fast

This trust content also tends to earn links and mentions, which helps your rankings.

## Step 4: Help homeowners understand cost

"How much does a new roof cost" is one of the most searched roofing questions. You don't have to publish a price list, but a page that honestly explains **what affects the price** is extremely useful:

- Roof size and pitch
- Material choice
- Removing old layers
- Repairs to the decking or structure underneath
- Access and safety requirements
- Gutters, flashing and ventilation

Finish with "the only way to know is an inspection" and a button to book one. It's helpful, honest and converts well.

## Step 5: Prepare for storm season before it arrives

After a big storm, searches for roof repairs spike in the affected area. Roofers who are ready capture most of that demand. Before the season:

- Publish a **storm damage page** that explains what to do after a storm, how inspections work and whether you help with insurance claims.
- Make sure your **hours and phone number** are correct everywhere.
- Prepare a **Google Business Profile post** you can publish quickly with an update about availability.
- Have a plan to **answer calls** when volume jumps.

Avoid alarmist "your roof is definitely damaged" messaging. It damages trust and attracts complaints.

## Step 6: Build a review engine

Roofing jobs are big and memorable, which makes them great for reviews. Ask after the final walk-through, when the customer can see the new roof. Send the review link by text the same day.

Reply to every review. For negative ones, stay calm, show you're fixing the problem and take the details offline. Our guide on [responding to negative Google reviews](/blog/how-to-respond-to-negative-google-reviews/) has examples you can adapt.

Don't offer anything in exchange for reviews, and ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Both are Google rules.

## Step 7: Earn links from trusted sources

Good link sources for roofers:

- Roofing manufacturers' certified contractor directories
- Roofing associations and local builders' associations
- Local news when you help a community building or charity
- Home builders, property managers and insurance-related businesses you partner with
- Reputable home service directories in your country

## Track the right numbers

Rankings are nice, but booked inspections pay the bills. Track:

- Calls and form enquiries from your website
- Calls and direction requests from your Google profile
- Which pages bring visitors, in Google Search Console
- How each new customer found you (just ask)

## Roofing SEO mistakes to avoid

- **Stock photos** of roofs you didn't build
- **Fake city addresses** or virtual offices to rank in other towns
- **Keyword-stuffed business names**
- **Thin "city pages"** with nothing local about them
- **Buying reviews or links**
- **Ignoring mobile users.** Many roofing searches happen on phones, sometimes from a driveway looking up at the damage

## Frequently asked questions

### Is roofing SEO worth it for a small roofing company?

Usually, yes. Roofing jobs are high-value, so even a few extra jobs a year from Google can cover the effort. Start with the free work, your Google profile and reviews, before paying anyone.

### Should roofers create pages for every city?

Only for areas where you can add genuinely local content, like real projects, photos and reviews from that area. Thin pages with only the city name changed rarely help.

## Roofing SEO checklist

- [ ] Google Business Profile verified, "Roofing contractor" category, service areas set
- [ ] Separate website pages for each roofing service
- [ ] Licenses, insurance, certifications and warranties shown clearly
- [ ] Project gallery with real photos
- [ ] An honest "what affects roof cost" page
- [ ] Storm damage page and storm-season plan ready
- [ ] Review request sent after every job, every review replied to
- [ ] Listed with manufacturers and associations
- [ ] Calls and enquiries tracked by source
