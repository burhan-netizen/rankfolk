Landscaping SEO is about showing up when homeowners search for things like **"landscaper near me", "patio installation [town]" or "lawn care service"**, and then convincing them with your work. Landscaping is one of the most visual trades, so your photos do a lot of the selling. Pair them with a complete Google Business Profile and a page for each service, and you have most of what you need.

Landscapers usually juggle two kinds of work: **recurring maintenance** (mowing, lawn care, garden maintenance) and **one-off projects** (patios, retaining walls, planting, irrigation, outdoor lighting). They're searched differently, so cover both.

## The searches landscapers need to show up for

| Type | Example searches | What wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maintenance | "lawn mowing service near me", "garden maintenance [town]" | Business Profile, reviews, service areas |
| Projects | "patio installation [town]", "retaining wall contractor", "landscape design" | Project pages with photos, service pages |
| Seasonal | "spring cleanup", "fall leaf removal", "snow removal" (if offered) | Seasonal pages, profile posts |
| Research | "pavers vs concrete patio", "best low-maintenance garden ideas" | Helpful guides with your photos |

Use the terms your customers use: "yard" and "lawn care" in the US, "garden" in the UK and Australia, "hardscaping" or "paving" depending on the area.

## Step 1: Build a strong Google Business Profile

- **Primary category:** the closest match, such as "Landscaper", "Landscape designer" or "Lawn care service". Add secondary categories only for services you really offer.
- **Service areas:** the towns you cover, and hide your home address if you work from home.
- **Services:** list each one with a short description.
- **Photos:** this is where landscapers can stand out. Upload before-and-after shots regularly, not all at once.
- **Posts:** seasonal offers and recent projects.

Our [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/) covers every section.

## Step 2: Turn your photos into project pages

Most landscaping websites have a gallery of unlabeled photos. A better approach is **project pages**:

- A title like "Sandstone patio and planting, Woodstock"
- Before and after photos
- What the customer wanted, what you did, materials used, how long it took
- A short quote from the customer, with permission

Project pages give Google real, specific content about what you do and where, and give customers proof they can picture in their own garden or yard. Name image files descriptively (`sandstone-patio-woodstock.jpg`) and compress them so pages load fast.

## Step 3: Create a page for each main service

For example:

- Lawn care and mowing
- Garden or yard maintenance
- Landscape design
- Patios and paving / hardscaping
- Retaining walls
- Planting and turf installation
- Irrigation
- Outdoor lighting
- Seasonal services (cleanups, leaf removal, snow removal)

Each page: what's included, how the process works, typical timeframes, what affects cost, photos from real jobs and FAQs.

## Step 4: Make it easy to get a quote

- Tap-to-call number at the top of every page
- A quote form that lets people upload a photo of their garden or yard
- Clear service areas
- For maintenance: explain how scheduling and recurring visits work

## Step 5: Get reviews from every customer

Maintenance customers are a great source of reviews because you see them often. Ask after a project finishes, or a few visits into a maintenance plan. Our [review request generator](/tools/review-request-generator/) creates the messages and a QR code for your invoices or van.

## Step 6: Plan for the seasons

Publish seasonal pages and Business Profile posts **before** each season starts: spring cleanups, summer irrigation, fall cleanups, and winter services if you offer them. Refresh the same pages each year instead of creating new ones.

## Step 7: Get mentioned beyond your site

- Supplier "find an installer" pages for paving, turf or irrigation brands you're certified with
- Local garden centers and builders you work with
- Apple Maps, Bing Places and reputable local directories
- Local news or community features about notable projects

## Landscaping SEO mistakes to avoid

- A gallery of unlabeled photos instead of project pages
- Huge, uncompressed photos that make the site slow on phones
- One page for every service
- No service areas listed
- Photos only on Instagram, not on your website or Business Profile

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Instagram enough for a landscaping business?

Instagram is great for showing work, but people searching Google for "landscaper near me" find Business Profiles and websites. Use your Instagram photos on your profile and project pages too.

### How do I get bigger landscaping projects from Google?

Project pages with detailed photos, landscape design and hardscaping service pages, and reviews that mention larger jobs. Bigger clients research more, so give them more to look at.

### How long does landscaping SEO take?

A complete Business Profile with regular photos and reviews can bring calls within weeks. Ranking your website for competitive searches usually takes months. Our [SEO for contractors guide](/blog/seo-for-contractors/) explains the bigger picture.
