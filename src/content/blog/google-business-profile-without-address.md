Yes, you can have a Google Business Profile without a storefront or public address. Google calls this a **service-area business**: a business that visits or delivers to customers, like plumbers, cleaners, mobile mechanics, dog walkers and most contractors. You enter your address so Google can verify you, then **hide it** and show the areas you serve instead.

In fact, if customers don't come to your address, Google requires you to hide it ([Google's service-area help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137)). Here's how to set it up properly, and the mistakes that get home-based businesses suspended.

## Who counts as a service-area business?

| Business setup | Profile type |
| --- | --- |
| You travel to customers and nobody visits your address | Service-area business (address hidden) |
| Customers visit your premises during staffed hours | Storefront business (address shown) |
| Customers visit you, and you also travel to them | Hybrid (address shown plus service areas) |
| You only work online, no in-person contact | Not eligible for a Business Profile |

Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say hybrid businesses can show an address and a service area, but the location must be staffed and able to receive customers during the hours shown.

## How to set up a profile as a service-area business

1. Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and start creating your profile.
2. Enter your business name exactly as customers know it (no extra keywords).
3. Choose your primary category.
4. When asked whether you have a location customers can visit, choose **No**.
5. Add your **service areas**: cities, postal codes or regions.
6. Add your phone number and website.
7. Enter your address for verification. It won't be shown publicly if you've said customers don't visit.
8. Complete verification.

Our full [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/) covers the rest of the profile.

## How to hide your address on an existing profile

If your address is already showing and it shouldn't be, Google's steps are:

1. Go to your Business Profile and select **Edit profile**.
2. Select **Location**.
3. Next to "Business location", select the edit icon.
4. Turn off **Show business address to customers**.
5. Save.

Once hidden, your profile shows your service area instead of a pin.

## How to set your service areas

Google lets you add **up to 20 service areas**, based on cities, postal codes or other regions. It also recommends your overall area shouldn't extend **farther than about two hours' driving time** from where you're based, though larger areas may be appropriate for some businesses.

Tips:

- Add the places you actually work most, not every town on the map.
- Use the names customers use (city names usually work better than obscure districts).
- Keep it realistic. Huge service areas look suspicious and don't make you rank everywhere.

## Will hiding my address hurt my ranking?

Hiding your address is the correct setup for a service-area business, and showing a home address customers aren't meant to visit breaks Google's rules. Google still knows where you're based for ranking purposes, and distance from the searcher is still one of its local ranking factors ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

In practice, that means you'll usually rank most strongly near your base. Setting a service area doesn't make you rank equally well across all of it. To compete further out, work on relevance and prominence: a complete profile, a strong website and lots of reviews.

## How to verify without a storefront

You still verify using your real address. Google decides the method. If you're asked for **video verification**, service-area businesses can show:

- Landmarks or signs near your business address (home-based businesses can show their neighborhood)
- The tools, equipment, branded vehicle or uniforms you use for work
- Proof of management, such as unlocking your branded van, performing your service, or showing a permit, invoice or utility bill matching your business name

See our step-by-step guide to [verifying your Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-video-verification/).

## Mistakes that get home-based businesses suspended

- **Using a virtual office or mailbox** as your address. Google doesn't allow them for service-area businesses unless the office is staffed during business hours.
- **Using a PO box.** Not allowed.
- **Showing your home address** when customers don't visit.
- **Creating multiple profiles** to appear in more towns. Google says to create only one profile per metropolitan area you serve, from a real base.
- **Adding town names to your business name.**
- **Using a call-center number** instead of a number that reaches your business directly.

## Making the most of a service-area profile

Without a pin on the map, other parts of your profile carry more weight:

- **Photos:** your vans, crew and finished jobs.
- **Services:** list every service with a short description.
- **Reviews:** ask every customer and reply to each one.
- **Posts:** share recent jobs, ideally mentioning the area ("New patio in Riverside this week").
- **Website:** a page per service and a clear list of areas you cover.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will Google show my home address if I hide it?

No. Once you've turned off "Show business address to customers", your profile shows your service areas instead. Google still uses your address behind the scenes for verification and to understand where you're based.

### Can I list a service-area business in several cities?

You can add up to 20 service areas to one profile. What you shouldn't do is create separate profiles for each city unless you genuinely have separate, staffed locations. Google says to create only one profile per metropolitan area you serve, and duplicates can lead to suspension.

### Can I use a co-working space or virtual office address?

A virtual office or mailing address where you don't actually operate isn't allowed. A co-working space can qualify only if it meets Google's conditions, such as clear signage, receiving customers during business hours and being staffed by your own team. For most home-based businesses, using your real address and hiding it is the correct setup.

### Why don't I rank across my whole service area?

Distance from the searcher is one of Google's ranking factors, so you'll usually rank best near where you're based. Your service area tells customers where you work; it doesn't guarantee visibility everywhere inside it. Reviews, a complete profile and a strong website help you compete further out.

### Can I change from a service-area business to a storefront later?

Yes. If you open premises where customers visit during staffed hours, update your profile to show the address. Google may ask you to verify again after a change like this.

## Checklist: Google Business Profile without a storefront

- [ ] Chose "No" for a location customers can visit
- [ ] Real business name, no keywords or towns added
- [ ] Specific primary category
- [ ] Up to 20 realistic service areas, within about two hours' drive
- [ ] Address entered for verification but hidden
- [ ] Profile verified
- [ ] No virtual office, PO box or duplicate profiles
- [ ] Services, photos and description completed
- [ ] Review requests going out after every job

Set up this way, a home-based business can compete in the map results just as well as one with a shopfront, and keep its home address private.
