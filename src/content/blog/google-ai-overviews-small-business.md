**AI Overviews** are the AI-written summaries that appear at the top of many Google searches. **AI Mode** is a separate, chat-style way of searching Google where you can ask longer questions and follow-ups. Both build their answers from web pages and other information Google has, and both show links to the sources they used.

For small businesses, the short version is: **people still need to hire someone, and Google still needs to show them who.** AI changes how answers are presented, but the businesses that get mentioned are mostly the ones that already do the basics of local SEO well. In 2026 Google also gave site owners two new things: a Search Console report that shows how often your pages appear in AI features, and a setting to opt out of them. This guide explains what's changed, what each setting does, and what to focus on.

## What AI Overviews and AI Mode actually do

- **AI Overviews** appear for some searches, usually questions ("how often should I service my boiler?") or comparisons. They summarize an answer and link to the pages used. Google says they only show when it thinks they add something to normal results, so many searches don't get one.
- **AI Mode** is built for longer questions that need reasoning or comparisons, such as planning a project or weighing up options. You reach it from the AI Mode tab or button in Google Search, and it also shows links.
- **Local results haven't gone away.** For searches like "electrician near me", Google still typically shows the map and Business Profiles, because people want a business they can call.

Google explains how site owners fit into both features in its guide to [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). One detail worth knowing: both features can use a "query fan-out" technique, running several related searches behind the scenes to build one answer. That means a single AI answer can pull from pages that rank for slightly different questions, not just the exact words someone typed.

### AI Overviews vs AI Mode at a glance

| | AI Overviews | AI Mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it appears | Top of normal Google results | Its own tab or chat-style view |
| When it shows | Only on some searches | Whenever someone chooses to use it |
| Typical questions | Quick "how" and "what" questions | Longer comparisons, planning, multi-part questions |
| Links to websites | Yes | Yes |
| Counted in Search Console | Yes | Yes |

## How it affects small businesses

### Informational searches: fewer clicks for simple answers

If someone searches "how to unblock a sink", the AI Overview may answer it on the spot. Pages built only to answer quick questions can lose clicks. That's a real change for blogs and DIY guides.

### Buying searches: still about you

When someone is ready to hire, they need a name, a phone number, reviews and confidence. That comes from your **Google Business Profile**, your **website** and what others say about you. AI answers can mention businesses, but they're drawing on the same information.

### Research before buying: a new chance to be mentioned

Bigger decisions ("is a heat pump worth it in the UK?", "composite vs timber decking") increasingly start with AI answers, and AI Mode is designed for exactly these longer questions. If your website explains them clearly, it can be one of the sources linked.

## What Google says you need to do

Google's guidance is direct: there are **no special requirements or special optimizations** needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. The same SEO best practices apply ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). In practice:

1. Your pages must be **indexed** and eligible to show a snippet in normal search. There are no extra technical requirements.
2. Googlebot must be **allowed to crawl** your site, in robots.txt and by your host or CDN.
3. Content should be **helpful, reliable and made for people**, as described in Google's guide to [creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
4. Important information should be in **text**, not only in images or PDFs.
5. Any **structured data** should match what's visible on the page.
6. Your **Business Profile** details should be accurate and up to date. Google names this directly in its AI features guide.

## 7 ways to stay visible as search changes

### 1. Make your Google Business Profile excellent

For local businesses this matters more than ever, because it's where the call happens. Complete every section, add real photos, keep your hours accurate and keep reviews coming. Start with our [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/).

### 2. Give each service its own clear page

AI answers and normal search both reward pages that clearly answer one need: "Heat pump installation in Bristol", with what's included, how it works and real FAQs.

### 3. Answer the questions customers ask before they buy

Price factors, how long jobs take, which option suits which home, what can go wrong. Write these from your experience. Generic answers are exactly what AI summarizes away. Specific, experienced answers are what it links to. Because of query fan-out, a page that answers the follow-up questions ("how long does it take?", "do I need a permit?") gives Google more reasons to use it.

### 4. Show real experience

Photos of your work, named reviews, case studies with details, your licenses and years in business. Google's guidance puts weight on experience and trustworthiness.

### 5. Collect reviews that mention the service and area

Reviews like "fixed our boiler in Leeds the same day" help both people and machines understand what you do and where.

### 6. Get mentioned on other trusted sites

Local news, trade associations, supplier "find an installer" pages and reputable directories. Mentions build the prominence Google uses for local rankings.

### 7. Track calls and enquiries, not just traffic

If traffic to your how-to articles dips but calls hold steady, you're fine. Watch your Business Profile performance (calls, direction requests, website clicks) and enquiries alongside Search Console.

## How to see your AI Overview and AI Mode visibility

Until mid-2026, you couldn't separate AI traffic in Search Console at all. Now there are two places to look:

- **The main Performance report** still includes clicks and impressions from AI features, mixed in with normal results under the "Web" search type ([Google's AI features guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).
- **The Generative AI performance report** is a separate view, found under the Performance section in Search Console. It shows **impressions** in AI Overviews and AI Mode, broken down by page, country, device and date ([Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139)). An impression here means a link to your site was shown in an AI feature.

Three things to know before you read too much into it:

1. **It shows impressions, not clicks.** The report lists impressions as its metric. Your clicks from AI features are still counted, but only inside the main Performance report.
2. **It may not appear for small sites.** Google says the report usually doesn't show when a site hasn't had enough impressions in AI features yet.
3. **Look at which pages appear.** If your cost guide or FAQ page shows up but your service pages don't, that tells you which kind of content AI answers are drawing on.

New to Search Console? Our guide to [setting up Google Search Console](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-search-console/) gets you started in about 15 minutes.

**A tip from client work:** before changing anything because of AI, export the last three months of your Performance report and note your monthly calls and enquiries. Then you have a baseline. Without one, it's very hard to tell whether a drop in clicks is AI, the season or something on your own site.

## Can you stop your content appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode?

Yes. Since August 31, 2026, every site in Search Console has a **Search generative AI** setting, under **Settings** ([Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024)). It has two main choices:

- **Include** (the default): your pages can appear as links in AI Overviews, AI Mode and AI features in Discover, and can be used to help ground AI answers.
- **Exclude**: your pages won't be linked in those features or used to build their answers, and you won't get traffic or impressions from them. Google says the change usually takes effect within a few days.

According to Google, excluding your site doesn't affect your rankings in other parts of Search. But content from other sites will still appear in those AI answers, and Google notes it may look similar to yours. Older options still exist too: `nosnippet` and similar controls limit how your pages show in all of Search, not just AI features, and the `Google-Extended` setting is about AI training, not about appearing in AI answers.

For most local businesses, **excluding is a bad trade.** A competitor gets linked instead of you, and you lose a way to be found by people researching a job before they hire. It can make sense for publishers whose income depends on page views, but a plumber, roofer or accountant usually wants every chance to be seen.

## When Google calls your business for a customer

In the US, Google can make automated calls to local businesses on behalf of searchers, for example to check the price and availability of a service or to book an appointment. Google says these calls are free, recorded for quality, limited in number and avoid late night and early morning hours. The feature isn't available in every state ([Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7690269)).

What this means in practice:

- **Answer clearly and quickly.** A call from Google asking about price and availability is a real customer request. If you share a quote, the customer gets it by email or text.
- **Train whoever answers the phone.** Make sure they know your typical price ranges and when you can come out.
- **You're in control.** In your Business Profile, go to **More**, then **Business Profile settings**, then **Advanced settings**, and find "Google automated calls and text messages" to turn customer bookings and inquiries on or off.
- **Watch for scams.** Google says it will never ask for payment, card details or passwords. A caller asking for money to keep you on Google isn't Google.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to pay to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode?

No. There's no way to pay for placement in the AI-generated answer. Ads can appear in separate, labeled positions.

### Will AI Overviews replace my website?

No. People still visit websites to check a business before calling, see photos and read reviews. Your site needs to make that decision easy.

### Should I turn off AI features in Search Console?

For most small businesses, no. Excluding your site removes you from AI answers but not your competitors, and it doesn't improve your normal rankings. Leave the default on and focus on being the best source for your area.

### Why can't I see the Generative AI performance report?

Usually because your site hasn't had enough impressions in AI features yet, which is common for newer or smaller sites. It can also mean your site has been set to exclude AI features. Check **Settings**, then **Search generative AI**.

### Does AI Mode change how I rank on Google Maps?

Google hasn't said that AI Mode changes how local rankings work. Map results still depend on relevance, distance and prominence, so the same work applies: a complete Business Profile, reviews and a clear website.

### Is "AI SEO" or "GEO" different from normal SEO?

You'll hear terms like GEO (generative engine optimization). For a small business, the work is almost entirely the same as good local SEO: accurate profiles, clear pages, real reviews and mentions. Google itself says no special optimization is required. Be wary of anyone selling a secret AI ranking trick.

### What about ChatGPT and other AI assistants?

They work differently from Google but rely on similar public information. See [how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT](/blog/how-to-get-your-business-recommended-by-chatgpt/).

## AI search checklist for small businesses

- [ ] Your key pages are indexed and Googlebot isn't blocked
- [ ] Every service has its own clear page with real FAQs and prices you actually charge (or the factors that affect them)
- [ ] Your Google Business Profile is complete, with accurate hours and recent photos
- [ ] You ask every happy customer for a review
- [ ] You've opened the Generative AI performance report (if it appears) and noted which pages show up
- [ ] Your Search generative AI setting is on "Include", unless you have a clear reason to exclude
- [ ] If you're in the US, whoever answers your phone knows how to handle a call from Google about price or availability
- [ ] You track calls and enquiries each month, not just website clicks

## Summary

- AI Overviews summarize answers at the top of Google. AI Mode is a chat-style search for longer questions.
- Simple how-to searches may send fewer clicks. Buying searches still lead to businesses.
- Google says no special optimization is needed: the same SEO basics apply.
- Search Console now shows your AI impressions in a separate report, and lets you opt out. Most local businesses should stay in.
- Focus on your Business Profile, clear service pages, experienced answers, reviews and mentions, and measure calls, not just traffic.
