To write website copy with AI that doesn't sound like AI, **don't ask it to "write a homepage". Ask it to interview you first**, then write from your answers, then edit hard. AI produces generic text when it has nothing specific to work with. Feed it your real knowledge and it becomes a fast, useful copywriter.

I write and review website copy for small businesses, and the difference between a generic AI page and a good one is almost always the input, not the tool. Here's the process I'd use.

## Step 1: Let the AI interview you

Instead of describing your business in one line, have the AI ask the questions a good copywriter would:

```
You're a copywriter for small local businesses. Interview me about my
business so you can write my website. Ask one question at a time, up
to 15 questions. Cover: what I do, where, who my best customers are,
what they're worried about before hiring, what makes us different,
how a typical job works, common questions, and proof (reviews,
guarantees, licences). Don't write anything until I say "go".
```

Answer in your own words, as if you were talking to a customer. Short, honest answers are perfect. This step takes 15 to 20 minutes and is the whole secret.

## Step 2: Agree the page plan

```
Based on my answers, suggest the pages my website needs, with one
sentence on the purpose of each. One page per main service.
```

Compare it with our guide to [what pages a small business website should have](/blog/what-pages-should-a-small-business-website-have/). Most service businesses need 5 to 8 pages.

## Step 3: Write one page at a time

Never ask for the whole site at once. For each page:

```
Write the [page name] page using only facts from my answers.
Rules:
- Headline says what we do and where
- Short paragraphs, plain English, reading age around 12
- Speak to the customer ("you"), not about us
- Include: [the sections you want]
- End with one clear call to action: [call / quote / book]
- Put [CHECK] next to anything you weren't sure about
- Never invent prices, numbers, awards or years in business
```

For service pages, a strong structure is: the problem, the solution, what's included, how it works, proof, FAQs, call to action.

## Step 4: Remove the "AI voice"

AI copy has tell-tale habits. Search your draft for these and cut or rewrite them:

| AI habit | Example | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Empty openers | "In today's fast-paced world..." | Start with the customer's problem |
| Vague superlatives | "unparalleled quality", "top-notch service" | Say what you actually do: "10-year guarantee" |
| Fancy words | "elevate", "seamless", "unlock", "tailored solutions" | Use the words your customers use |
| Lists of three adjectives | "reliable, affordable and professional" | Prove one with a fact |
| Over-polished sentences | Every sentence the same length and rhythm | Read it out loud; vary it |
| Generic FAQs | "Why choose us?" | Real questions customers ask on the phone |

A quick trick: ask the AI *"Rewrite this as I'd say it to a customer on the phone"*, then compare with how you'd actually say it.

## Step 5: Add what only you can

This is what makes the page yours and builds trust:

- **Specific details:** areas, brands you use, typical timeframes, what's included
- **Real photos** of your team and your work
- **Named reviews,** with permission
- **Your opinion:** "For most older homes we recommend X, because..."
- **Honest limits:** "We don't do commercial work" saves everyone time

## Step 6: Check it like a customer

Read every page on your phone and ask:

- [ ] Within five seconds, do I know what they do and where?
- [ ] Is there anything here every competitor could also say? (Cut or make it specific.)
- [ ] Is every fact true and checked?
- [ ] Is the next step obvious, with a tappable phone number?
- [ ] Does it sound like us?

## Step 7: Write the page titles and descriptions

```
Write a page title (under 60 characters) and meta description (under
155 characters) for this page. Title format: [Service] in [Area] |
[Business name]. Description: what, where, one reason to choose us,
and a call to action.
```

Then check how it looks with our free [Google search preview tool](/tools/google-search-preview/).

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing the first draft.** It will read like everyone else's site.
- **Letting AI invent facts.** Always use the [CHECK] instruction and remove anything unconfirmed.
- **Copying the same page for every town.** Google treats near-duplicate pages as low quality. See [is AI content bad for SEO?](/blog/is-ai-content-bad-for-seo/)
- **Too much text.** Customers skim. Shorter, specific pages convert better than long generic ones.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which AI tool is best for website copy?

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot can all do this well. The method above matters far more than the tool.

### Will Google penalize AI-written website copy?

Google rewards helpful content however it's produced. Edited, accurate, specific copy is fine. Mass-produced, low-value pages are the risk.

### Should I hire a copywriter instead?

If your website is your main source of leads and your budget allows, a good copywriter (or a web designer who writes) is worth it. AI with this method gets you a solid result if you're doing it yourself. ## Summary

1. Let the AI interview you before it writes anything
2. Plan the pages, then write one page at a time
3. Forbid invented facts and mark anything uncertain
4. Strip out the AI voice and add your own details, photos and opinions
5. Check every page on your phone, like a customer would
