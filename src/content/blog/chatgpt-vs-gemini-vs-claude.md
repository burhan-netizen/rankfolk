For most small businesses, **the best AI assistant is the one that fits the tools you already use.** If your business runs on Gmail, Google Docs and Google Sheets, start with **Gemini**, because it works inside those apps and comes with most Google Workspace business plans. If you want one all-round assistant for writing, photos, voice and quick research, start with **ChatGPT**. If most of your work is writing and editing longer documents, like quotes, proposals, policies and website pages, try **Claude**, which many people find writes in a calmer, more natural voice.

All three have free versions that are good enough to test on your own work, and all three now cost about the same at the first paid level. So don't agonize over the choice. Pick one based on the table below, use it for two weeks on real jobs, and only switch if it keeps letting you down.

## The quick answer: which one fits your business?

| If this sounds like you | Start with | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We live in Gmail, Google Docs and Sheets" | Gemini | Built into Google Workspace, so it can draft emails and summarize threads where you already work |
| "I want one app on my phone for everything" | ChatGPT | The widest set of features: voice, photos, image creation, search and file uploads |
| "I write a lot of quotes, proposals and long emails" | Claude | Strong at long documents and at keeping a consistent, natural tone |
| "We use Microsoft 365 (Outlook, Word, Excel)" | ChatGPT or Claude | Both offer add-ins for Microsoft apps on paid plans; Microsoft's own Copilot is also worth a look |
| "I just want to try AI for free" | Any of the three | The free plans handle review replies, social posts and emails well |

The differences between them are smaller than the internet suggests. For everyday small business jobs, the quality of what you get out depends far more on what you put in (your facts, your tone, your examples) than on which logo is at the top of the screen. ## ChatGPT: the all-rounder

ChatGPT, made by OpenAI, is the one most people have heard of, and it's the easiest place to start if you've never used AI before.

**Good at:**

- Everyday writing: emails, social posts, review replies, job ads
- Taking a photo and doing something with it, like "write a Facebook post about this finished bathroom" or "what's this part called?"
- Voice chats, which are handy in the van or on site
- Creating simple images and graphics
- Searching the web and summarizing what it finds

**Watch out for:**

- Its writing can sound generic and "salesy" unless you give it your tone and facts
- The free plan has limits on uploads, image creation and the more advanced features, and OpenAI notes that its lower-cost Go plan may include ads ([ChatGPT pricing](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/))

**Best for:** owners who want one assistant for a bit of everything, mostly on their phone.

## Gemini: best if you already use Google

Gemini is Google's AI assistant. You can use it as a standalone chat app, but its real advantage for a small business is that it's built into Google Workspace: Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive and Meet.

**Good at:**

- Summarizing long email threads and drafting replies inside Gmail
- Helping with documents and spreadsheets without copying and pasting between apps
- Taking notes in Google Meet calls
- Working with files you already keep in Google Drive

Google says Gemini and its business AI features are included in Google Workspace plans, and that your company data in Workspace is not used to train its models or for ads ([Google Workspace AI](https://workspace.google.com/lp/solutions/ai/), [Workspace privacy hub](https://support.google.com/a/answer/15706919)). If you already pay for Workspace for your business email, you may already have Gemini and not be using it.

**Watch out for:**

- The personal Gemini app (on a free Gmail account) and Gemini for business accounts are not the same product, and they have different privacy settings
- Some features are only available in certain countries or languages, so check what your plan includes where you are

**Best for:** businesses that already run on Google Workspace, and anyone who spends a big part of the day in Gmail.

## Claude: best for writing and longer documents

Claude is made by Anthropic. It has fewer bells and whistles for casual phone use than ChatGPT, but it's popular with people who write a lot.

**Good at:**

- Long documents: proposals, quotes with scope notes, policies, staff handbooks, website pages
- Reading a long file you upload (a contract, a tender document, a supplier's terms) and pulling out what matters
- Following instructions about tone, like "plain English, no exclamation marks, no hype"
- Editing your own writing without making it sound like a robot

**Watch out for:**

- Usage limits on the free plan reset every few hours, so heavy users can hit them quickly ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing))
- It's less of an "everything app" for creating images than ChatGPT

**Best for:** owners and office managers who spend a lot of time on written work and want it to sound like a person wrote it.

**Do you need to pay at all?** If you use AI a few times a week for review replies and social posts, the free plans are usually enough. Upgrade when you keep hitting limits, or when you want to save your business details permanently in a project so you don't have to paste them every time.

## Privacy: what happens to what you type

This matters more for a business than for personal use, because it's easy to paste in a customer's name, address or a quote with your prices.

- **Personal plans (free and individual paid plans):** ChatGPT and Claude both list model training as something you can opt out of on their individual plans, according to their pricing pages. In plain terms, check the settings and turn off "improve the model" or similar if you don't want your chats used.
- **Business plans:** Google says Workspace data isn't used to train Gemini models. Anthropic says Claude Team doesn't train on your content by default ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). OpenAI explains how business data is handled on its [enterprise privacy page](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/).
- **Whatever you use:** don't paste customers' personal details, card numbers or passwords into any AI chat. Replace names with "the customer" and addresses with "a house in [town]". You'll get the same quality of answer.

If you have staff, a one-page rule ("what we never paste into AI") is worth more than any setting.

## The same job in all three: what to expect

Here's what to expect when you give the same everyday jobs to each tool:

| Task | What usually happens |
| --- | --- |
| Reply to a 5-star Google review | All three do this well. Differences are tiny. |
| Calm reply to an angry review | All three are good if you tell them "don't argue, invite them to call". Claude tends to sound least scripted. |
| Facebook post from a job photo | ChatGPT and Gemini handle photos smoothly. All three need you to cut the hype. |
| Service page for your website | All three write a decent first draft. None of them know your prices, licenses or areas unless you tell them. |
| Summarize a long email chain | Gemini is easiest if the emails are in Gmail. Otherwise paste the thread into any of them. |
| Read a 30-page contract and list the risks | Claude and ChatGPT both handle long files well. Always have a professional check anything legal. |

The point: for most everyday jobs, you won't lose much by picking "the wrong one". You lose far more by giving any of them vague instructions.

**A tip from client work:** when we set owners up with AI, the biggest improvement never comes from switching tools. It comes from writing a short "business brief" (what you do, where, your tone, words you never use, and "never invent prices or claims") and saving it in the tool's project or custom instructions feature. Owners who skip this usually decide AI "sounds fake". Owners who do it usually keep using it.

## How to choose in 3 steps

1. **Start with what you already pay for.** If you have Google Workspace, open Gemini in Gmail first. If you don't, open the free version of ChatGPT or Claude.
2. **Test it on three real jobs this week.** For example: reply to your last three reviews, write one social post from a recent job photo, and rewrite one page of your website. Use your own facts, not made-up examples.
3. **Only pay or switch if it keeps failing.** If the free plan keeps running out, upgrade. If the writing keeps missing your tone after you've given it a business brief, try one of the other two on the same jobs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is better, ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude?

There's no single winner for small businesses. ChatGPT is the best all-rounder, Gemini is the best fit if you use Google Workspace, and Claude is a strong choice for writing and long documents. For everyday tasks like review replies and social posts, all three do a good job.

### Can I use all three for free?

Yes. ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude all have free plans. The free versions have usage limits and fewer advanced features, but they're enough to test each one on your real work before paying.

### Is Gemini free with Google Workspace?

Google says Gemini and its built-in AI features are included in Google Workspace business plans, so if you already pay for Workspace you may not need a separate subscription. Check your plan in the Workspace admin console, as features vary by plan and country.

### Is it safe to put customer information into AI tools?

Avoid it where you can. Business plans from all three providers offer stronger data protections than personal plans, but the safest habit is not to paste personal details at all. Swap names and addresses for placeholders.

### Will using AI-written content hurt my Google rankings?

Not by itself. Google cares whether content is helpful, not whether AI helped write it. Publishing lots of unedited, generic pages is the risk. Our guide [is AI content bad for SEO?](/blog/is-ai-content-bad-for-seo/) explains where the line is.

### Does it matter which AI tool my customers use?

It matters for getting found, not for doing your own work. Customers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini and other assistants for recommendations. Our guide to [getting your business recommended by ChatGPT](/blog/how-to-get-your-business-recommended-by-chatgpt/) covers what helps you show up.
