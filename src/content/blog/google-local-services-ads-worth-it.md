**Google Local Services Ads (LSAs)** are ads that appear at the very top of Google for local service searches like "plumber near me" or "electrician in Chicago". Unlike normal Google Ads, you don't pay per click. **You pay per lead**: when a customer calls, messages or books you through the ad.

For many contractors and home service businesses, LSAs can be worth it, because you're paying for real contacts rather than website visits. But they're not right for everyone, and they work best alongside a strong Google Business Profile and good reviews. Here's how they work, so you can decide.

## How Local Services Ads work

Google's [Local Services Ads overview](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7549347) explains the basics:

- You tell Google your services, hours and service area.
- Google creates a profile for your ad, showing your business name, location, star rating and review count, hours and photos.
- When someone clicks your ad, they see your profile and can call or message you. Calls go through a tracking number that forwards to your phone.
- Businesses that pass Google's checks can show a "Google Verified" badge in most categories.
- You only pay when a customer gets in touch through the ad.

LSAs aren't available in every country or every business category. Check the [Local Services Ads site](https://ads.google.com/local-services-ads/) to see if your trade and area are eligible.

## What counts as a lead (and what you pay for)

According to [Google's pay-per-lead help page](https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/7195435), you're generally charged when:

- You answer a call, or call the customer back and speak with them
- You miss a call during business hours and the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds
- You receive a message, email or voicemail through the ad
- You receive a booking request (where available)

You're generally **not** charged for:

- Spam, duplicate or invalid leads that Google identifies when the customer first gets in touch
- Missed calls that end within 20 seconds
- Repeat contact from the same customer within 15 days of a charged lead

Google also reassesses charged leads over time and may automatically credit ones it later decides were low quality ([source](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7435486)).

## How much do Local Services Ads cost?

Google doesn't publish fixed prices. Lead costs vary by location, job type, lead type (calls vs messages) and how you set your bidding. You set an **average weekly budget**, and Google caps your spending at a monthly maximum based on that budget ([source](https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/7195435)).

The best way to judge cost is your own numbers after a trial period: total spend divided by jobs won.

## Local Services Ads vs Google Ads

| | Local Services Ads | Google Ads (search) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You pay for | Leads (calls, messages, bookings) | Clicks to your website |
| Position | Usually at the very top for eligible searches | Top and bottom of results |
| Setup effort | Lower: profile, verification, budget | Higher: keywords, ads, landing pages |
| Control | Less (services, areas, budget, hours) | More (keywords, ad text, bids, schedules) |
| Needs a website? | No, but it helps | Yes, a good landing page is essential |
| Reviews matter? | Yes, rating and count show in the ad | Less directly |

Many businesses use both: LSAs for urgent, high-intent calls, and Google Ads for specific services or areas LSAs don't cover well.

## When LSAs are usually worth it

- You're in an eligible home service trade with good job values
- You can **answer calls quickly**, since leads that go to voicemail are wasted money
- You already have a decent number of good reviews
- You want leads now while your SEO builds
- You can handle more work in your service area

## When they might not be worth it

- Your average job is small and leads cost more than the job margin
- You can't answer the phone during business hours
- You have very few or poor reviews (your rating shows right in the ad)
- You're in a category or area where LSAs aren't available
- You're already fully booked from free sources

## How to get better results from LSAs

1. **Answer every call.** Many charged leads come from calls you pick up, and a missed call over 20 seconds can still be charged.
2. **Get more reviews.** Your rating and review count are shown in the ad, so they affect whether people choose you. See [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).
3. **Set accurate hours.** Only show availability when someone can answer.
4. **Only select services and areas you want.** Unwanted job types waste budget.
5. **Rate your leads** in the dashboard, so Google learns what a good lead looks like for you.
6. **Track to the job, not the lead.** Write down which leads you quoted and which you won.

## How LSAs fit with your other marketing

LSAs are rented visibility: the calls stop when the budget stops. Meanwhile, your Google Business Profile, reviews, website and SEO keep working without a per-lead cost. The healthiest approach for most service businesses is to use LSAs to fill the schedule while building free sources over time. ## Should you try Local Services Ads?

Run the numbers:

1. What's your average job value and profit margin?
2. What percentage of leads do you usually win?
3. How much could you afford to pay per lead and still make good money?

Then run a trial for a month or two with a modest weekly budget, track every lead to a won or lost job, and decide based on your actual cost per won job.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website to use Local Services Ads?

No. Local Services Ads lead to a Google-hosted profile built from the information you provide, and customers can call or message you from there. A good website still helps, because many people check a business out before calling.

### Can I choose which jobs I get leads for?

You choose the service types and areas you want to appear for, and you can turn job types on or off. Keep this list tight. Paying for leads on jobs you don't want to do is one of the most common ways LSA budgets get wasted.

### What happens if I get a bad lead?

Google says spam and invalid leads identified at first contact aren't charged, and charged leads are reassessed over time, with low-quality ones sometimes credited automatically. You can also rate leads in your dashboard to help improve future lead quality. Credit rules vary by industry and region, so check the current help pages for your account.

### Do reviews affect Local Services Ads?

Your star rating and review count are shown in the ad, so they influence which business customers choose. A strong, steady flow of genuine reviews makes your ads more effective.

### Can I pause Local Services Ads?

Yes. You can pause your ads, for example when you're fully booked or on holiday, and turn them back on later. Pausing when you can't take work is better than paying for leads you'll have to turn away.

## Summary

- LSAs appear at the top of Google for eligible local service searches.
- You pay per lead, not per click.
- Lead costs vary; set a weekly budget and track cost per won job.
- Answering calls quickly and having strong reviews make a big difference.
- Use LSAs alongside free sources, not instead of them.
