It's a fair question. Customers can find you on Google Maps, message you on Instagram or Facebook, and read your reviews without ever visiting a website. So why pay for one?

The short answer: **yes, most small businesses still need a website.** But not because "every business should have one". It's because a good website does specific jobs that social media and your Google profile can't.

## What a website does that social media can't

### You own it

Your Instagram page, Facebook page and Google profile all live on someone else's platform. They can change the rules, limit your reach, or suspend your account, and you have little say. Your website and domain belong to you.

### It helps you rank on Google

Google looks at your website when deciding where to rank you, both in regular search results and on the map. A business with a clear, fast website that explains its services and service areas usually has an easier time ranking than one without.

A website also lets you rank for searches your Google profile alone won't cover, like "how much does a new roof cost" or "do I need a permit for a deck".

### It builds trust before the first call

Many customers check you out before they pick up the phone, especially for bigger jobs. A professional website with real photos, reviews and clear prices or price ranges answers their questions and makes you look established. No website, or an outdated one, can quietly send them to a competitor.

### It works while you're busy

A good website answers common questions, shows your past work and collects quote requests around the clock, so you spend less time repeating yourself on the phone.

## When you might not need one (yet)

There are a few cases where a website can wait:

- You're fully booked through referrals and don't want more work right now
- You're testing a side business and want to validate it first
- You sell mainly through a marketplace like Etsy or Amazon, where customers already shop

Even then, setting up a free [Google Business Profile](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/) is worth doing today.

## What a good small business website needs

You don't need a huge, expensive site. You need one that turns visitors into calls. Focus on these:

1. **A clear headline.** Within five seconds, visitors should know what you do and where. "Roofing repairs and replacements in Brisbane" beats "Welcome to our website".
2. **A visible phone number and call button.** Especially on mobile, where most local searches happen. One tap should start a call.
3. **A page for each main service.** This helps visitors find what they need and helps Google understand what you offer.
4. **Your service areas.** List the towns or suburbs you cover.
5. **Real photos of your work and team.** They build far more trust than stock photos.
6. **Reviews and proof.** Show testimonials, review ratings, licences and insurance where relevant.
7. **Fast loading.** A slow site loses visitors before it even appears. Keep images compressed and avoid heavy page builders where you can.
8. **A simple contact form.** Ask only for what you need: name, phone, and what the job is.

## How much does a small business website cost?

It ranges widely:

- **DIY website builders** (like Wix or Squarespace) cost a monthly subscription and your time.
- **Freelancers and small studios** usually charge a one-time fee for a custom site, plus optional ongoing care.
- **Agencies** charge more, but often include strategy, copywriting and SEO.

The right choice depends on your budget and how much your time is worth. A site that brings in even one or two extra jobs a month usually pays for itself quickly.

## The bottom line

Social media and Google Maps are great for being discovered. A website is where you turn that interest into calls, on a platform you own. For most small businesses, the best setup is all three working together: a complete Google Business Profile, active social pages, and a simple, fast website that makes it easy to get in touch.
