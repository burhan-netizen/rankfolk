For most small businesses: **Wix is the easiest all-rounder for DIY, Squarespace is best if design and a polished look matter most, and WordPress is best if you want maximum flexibility and are happy to maintain it (or pay someone to).** All three can rank on Google and bring in customers. The platform matters far less than the content you put on it.

This comparison focuses on what actually matters to a small business owner: how easy it is, how it looks, SEO, ongoing costs, maintenance and who owns what.

## Quick comparison

| | Wix | Squarespace | WordPress (self-hosted) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | All-in-one website builder | All-in-one website builder | Open-source software you install on hosting |
| Ease of use | Very beginner-friendly | Beginner-friendly, a little more structured | Steeper learning curve |
| Design | Many templates, very flexible layouts | Polished, design-led templates | Unlimited, depends on theme and builder |
| Flexibility | Good, with an app market | Good for most small sites | Highest, with thousands of plugins |
| Hosting | Included | Included | You arrange it separately |
| Maintenance | Handled for you | Handled for you | You (or your developer) handle updates, backups, security |
| Moving away later | Hard to move the site itself | Hard to move the site itself | Easiest to move between hosts |
| Best for | DIY owners who want lots of control | Design-focused businesses, portfolios | Growing businesses, custom needs |

Note: "WordPress" here means self-hosted WordPress (from WordPress.org). WordPress.com is a separate hosted service with its own plans.

## Wix: easiest for DIY

**Good for:** owners who want to build their own site quickly with drag-and-drop editing.

**Pros**
- Very easy to get started
- Lots of templates for different industries
- Hosting, security and updates included
- App market for bookings, forms, stores and more

**Cons**
- Total freedom in layout can lead to messy designs if you're not careful
- Moving your site to another platform later is difficult
- Adding many apps can slow your site down

## Squarespace: best for a polished look

**Good for:** businesses where visuals matter: photographers, designers, restaurants, salons, boutiques.

**Pros**
- Beautiful, consistent templates that are hard to make look bad
- Hosting, security and updates included
- Built-in tools for blogs, simple stores, bookings and email marketing (some as paid add-ons)

**Cons**
- Less layout freedom than Wix or WordPress
- Fewer third-party add-ons
- Moving away later is difficult

## WordPress: most flexible

**Good for:** businesses that want room to grow, specific features or a developer-built site.

**Pros**
- Huge choice of themes and plugins
- You can host it almost anywhere and move hosts later
- Easy to add advanced features: bookings, memberships, multiple locations, complex forms
- Huge community and lots of developers to choose from

**Cons**
- You're responsible for updates, backups and security, or need to pay someone
- Too many plugins can make sites slow and fragile
- Steeper learning curve for beginners
- Security problems happen mainly on poorly maintained sites. Our article on the [October 2026 PHP security flaw](/blog/php2shell-wordpress-security-small-business/) shows why maintenance matters.

## Which is best for SEO?

All three let you do the SEO basics a small business needs:

- Custom page titles and meta descriptions
- Clean page addresses (URLs)
- Automatic sitemaps
- Mobile-friendly templates
- Connecting Google Search Console

The difference is mostly in flexibility. WordPress gives the most control through SEO plugins and custom code. Wix and Squarespace cover the essentials well for most local businesses.

What actually decides your rankings: a page for each service, clear titles, useful content, a fast site, and a strong Google Business Profile with reviews. See our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-small-business/) for those.

## What about cost?

We don't list prices here because they change regularly and vary by country. Instead, compare total cost of ownership:

| Cost | Wix and Squarespace | WordPress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform | Monthly or yearly subscription | Software is free |
| Hosting | Included | Separate hosting plan |
| Domain | Sometimes included for the first year, then renewed | Bought separately |
| Themes and add-ons | Some free, some paid | Many free, premium ones often paid yearly |
| Maintenance | Included | Your time, or a paid care plan |
| Email | Usually a separate subscription | Usually a separate subscription |

Check each platform's current pricing page, and add up a full year including renewals and add-ons.

## Ownership: the question people forget

- **Your domain name:** always register it in your own name, whichever platform you use.
- **Wix and Squarespace:** you own your content, but the site design and structure live on their platform. If you leave, you usually rebuild.
- **WordPress:** you can download your whole site and move it to another host.

## Which should you choose?

| Your situation | Best fit |
| --- | --- |
| You want to build it yourself this weekend | Wix |
| You care most about looking polished | Squarespace |
| You want room to grow, or a developer will build it | WordPress |
| You hate maintenance and updates | Wix or Squarespace |
| You need unusual features or integrations | WordPress |
| You mainly sell products online | Consider Shopify, too |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is WordPress better than Wix for SEO?

WordPress offers more control, especially with SEO plugins, but Wix covers the essentials most local businesses need. Your content, service pages, page titles and Google Business Profile will influence rankings far more than the platform.

### Can I move my site from Wix or Squarespace to WordPress later?

You can, but it usually means rebuilding the design and moving content by hand or with import tools. Keep your domain in your own name so you can switch platforms without losing your web address.

### Which platform is best for a contractor website?

Any of the three can work well. Choose Wix or Squarespace if you want to build it yourself without maintenance, or WordPress if a developer is building it and you want flexibility. What matters most is having a page for each service, real project photos and a tappable phone number.

### Do I need to pay for a premium theme or template?

Not necessarily. Many free templates and themes are perfectly good. Pay for a premium option only if it gives you something you need, such as better speed, support or a specific feature.

## Summary

- **Wix:** easiest DIY, very flexible layouts, maintenance handled.
- **Squarespace:** best-looking templates, simpler, maintenance handled.
- **WordPress:** most flexible and portable, but needs ongoing care.
- All three can rank well. Content and your Google profile matter more than the platform.
- Compare total yearly cost and who handles maintenance, not just the headline price.
