**Local SEO** is everything you do to show up when people nearby search for what you sell: "dentist near me", "landscaper in Auckland", "best coffee in Halifax". It covers your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews and how your business appears across the web.

If your customers come from one town, city or region, local SEO matters more than any other kind of marketing you can do for free. And most of it you can do yourself, in an afternoon a month. Here's the full process, step by step.

## How local search works (in one minute)

When someone searches with local intent, Google usually shows a map with a few businesses, then normal website results. Google says the map results are ranked on three things ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)):

- **Relevance:** how well your listing matches the search
- **Distance:** how close you are to the searcher, or to the location in the search
- **Prominence:** how well known you are, including reviews, ratings and links from other sites

You can't move your business closer to everyone. But relevance and prominence are almost entirely in your hands.

## Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

This is the foundation. If you haven't created one, start with our [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/). If you have one, audit it against this list:

- **Business name:** exactly as it appears on your signage and paperwork. No extra keywords or locations. Google's [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) don't allow them, and it's a common reason for suspension.
- **Primary category:** the most specific one that describes what you *are*. Add a few secondary categories only if they're genuinely true.
- **Address or service area:** if customers don't visit you, hide your address and set a service area instead.
- **Hours:** accurate, including holiday hours.
- **Services:** list each one, with a short description.
- **Description:** plain, honest, mentions what you do and where.
- **Photos:** real ones. Your work, your team, your vehicles, your premises.

## Step 2: Make your NAP consistent everywhere

NAP stands for **Name, Address, Phone**. Search engines cross-check your business details across the web. If one directory says "Smith Roofing Ltd, 12 High St" and another says "Smith Roofers, Unit 3, High Street", it creates doubt.

Pick one exact version and use it on:

- Your website (usually in the footer and on the contact page)
- Your Google Business Profile
- Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn
- Apple Maps (Apple Business Connect) and Bing Places
- Any directories you're listed on

You don't need to be on hundreds of directories. Get the main ones right and fix any old listings with outdated details.

## Step 3: Build location-aware service pages

Your website should make it obvious **what you do and where you do it**. That means:

- One page per main service, not one page listing everything
- Your town, city or region mentioned naturally in headings and text
- A clear list of the areas you serve
- Your address (if customers visit) and phone number on every page

Should you create a page for every suburb? Only if you have something genuinely different to say about each one, such as local projects, photos and reviews from that area. Twenty near-identical pages with the town name swapped out is thin content, and it rarely helps.

## Step 4: Get reviews every week, not once a year

Reviews are one of the strongest local signals, and they're the main thing customers compare. What helps:

- **A steady flow.** Ten reviews spread over six months beats ten in one week then nothing.
- **Detail.** Reviews that mention the service and the area help both customers and Google.
- **Replies.** Google recommends responding to reviews, and it shows future customers you care.

Google gives you a review link and QR code inside your profile. Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/) covers what to say and when. One important rule: **never offer anything in exchange for a review**, and don't only ask the people you think will be happy. Both are against Google's [review policies](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114).

## Step 5: Earn local links and mentions

Prominence also comes from other websites mentioning you. Good local sources include:

- Business associations and chambers of commerce
- Suppliers, manufacturers and franchise networks ("find an installer" pages)
- Local news coverage of a project, milestone or community effort
- Sponsorships of local teams, events or charities
- Trade bodies and accreditation schemes

Quality beats quantity. One link from your local council's business directory or a respected trade association is worth more than fifty from random sites.

## Step 6: Post updates and photos on your profile

Google Business Profile lets you publish **posts**: updates, offers and events. Posts older than six months are archived unless you set dates, according to [Google's help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7662907). A simple habit of posting a recent job photo with a sentence about it every week or two keeps your profile fresh and gives searchers something to look at.

## Step 7: Track what's working

You don't need expensive software. Use free tools:

| Tool | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile performance | Calls, direction requests, website clicks and searches that found you |
| Google Search Console | Which searches show your website and how often people click |
| Google Analytics | What visitors do on your website |
| Your phone and inbox | Ask every new customer how they found you |

Set up Search Console with our [Search Console setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-search-console/) if you haven't already.

## Local SEO mistakes that hold businesses back

- **Keyword stuffing your Google business name.** It can get you suspended.
- **Using a virtual office or PO box** to fake a location. Google doesn't allow either.
- **Setting a huge service area.** Google recommends not going beyond roughly two hours' drive from your base.
- **Creating duplicate profiles** for the same business.
- **A website with no service pages** or no location information.
- **Ignoring negative reviews.** A calm, helpful reply often wins more trust than the review loses.

## How long will local SEO take?

Fixing your profile and getting reviews can show results in weeks, especially in smaller towns. Ranking a website in a competitive city takes longer, often many months. The businesses that win local search are rarely the cleverest, just the most consistent.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the difference between local SEO and regular SEO?

Regular SEO aims to rank a website for searches anywhere. Local SEO focuses on searches with a location, including the map results, so it puts much more weight on your Google Business Profile, reviews and local relevance.

### Can I do local SEO myself?

Yes. Most of the highest-impact work, such as completing your Google profile, getting reviews and writing service pages, needs knowledge of your business more than technical skills.

## Your monthly local SEO routine

Once the setup is done, this is all it takes to keep improving:

- [ ] Ask every happy customer for a review, and reply to every review
- [ ] Post one or two updates or job photos on your Google profile
- [ ] Add new photos of recent work
- [ ] Check your hours, especially before holidays
- [ ] Look at Search Console: which searches are growing, which pages need work
- [ ] Improve or add one service page
- [ ] Look for one new local link or mention

An hour or two a month, done every month, will beat a big one-off effort every time.
