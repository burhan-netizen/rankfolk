SEO for electricians is about one thing: **being one of the first electricians someone sees when they search Google, and making it easy for them to call.** Most of that happens in the map results, powered by your free Google Business Profile, backed up by a website with a clear page for each type of work you do.

Electrical work has two very different kinds of customer: the emergency ("power's out", "breaker keeps tripping") and the planned project (EV charger, rewire, panel upgrade, lighting). Good electrician SEO covers both. Here's how.

## The searches electricians need to show up for

| Type | Example searches | What wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | "emergency electrician near me", "power outage electrician" | Business Profile, phone answered, open hours |
| General | "electrician [town]", "local electrician" | Business Profile, reviews, homepage |
| Service-specific | "EV charger installation [town]", "panel upgrade", "house rewire cost" | Dedicated service pages |
| Research | "how much does a rewire cost", "do I need a permit for an EV charger" | Helpful guides on your website |

Your Search Console data (see [how to check where you rank](/blog/how-to-check-google-ranking/)) will show which of these you already appear for.

## Step 1: Set up your Google Business Profile properly

- **Primary category:** "Electrician". Add secondary categories only for work you really do, such as "Lighting contractor" or "Electric vehicle charging station contractor" if offered in your country.
- **Service areas:** list the towns and areas you actually cover. If you work from home, hide your address.
- **Services:** add each one (EV chargers, rewiring, panel upgrades, fault finding, inspections) with a short description.
- **Hours:** if you offer emergency call-outs, make your hours reflect it honestly.
- **Photos:** neat panels, finished installs, your van and team. Tidy work is a selling point in this trade.

Our [Google Business Profile setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-business-profile/) walks through every section, and our [verification video checklist](/tools/verification-video-checklist/) helps you pass verification first time.

## Step 2: Show your licence and insurance everywhere

Electrical work is regulated in most countries, and customers worry about safety. Put your licence or registration details, insurance and any trade body memberships on your website (footer and About page) and mention them in your profile description. It builds trust and answers a question people have before they call.

## Step 3: Build a page for each type of work

One "Services" page listing everything rarely ranks for anything. Create a page for each main service, for example:

- EV charger installation
- Panel or consumer unit upgrades
- Rewiring
- Lighting installation (indoor and outdoor)
- Fault finding and repairs
- Safety inspections and certificates
- Commercial electrical work (if you do it)

Each page should cover what's included, how the job works, typical timeframes, what affects the price, photos of real jobs and FAQs. Our guide to [writing website copy with AI](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-with-ai/) shows a fast way to draft these from your own knowledge.

## Step 4: Make calling you effortless

- Phone number at the top of every page, as a **tap-to-call link**
- A short quote form: name, phone, postcode or zip, what they need, optional photo
- "Emergency call-outs available" stated clearly, if you offer them
- Fast loading on phones

Run your homepage through our [free website audit](/tools/website-audit/) to check the basics.

## Step 5: Get reviews that mention the job and area

Reviews are one of the factors Google uses for local rankings, and they're what customers read before calling. Ask every customer, ideally the same day. Reviews like "installed our EV charger in Bristol, very tidy" help both people and Google. Our [review request generator](/tools/review-request-generator/) writes the message and makes a QR code for your van or invoices.

## Step 6: Write helpful guides for planned jobs

People research before bigger projects. Short, honest guides from your experience can bring in those searches and build trust:

- "How long does a house rewire take?"
- "Do I need a panel upgrade for an EV charger?"
- "Signs your wiring needs replacing"
- "What affects the cost of an EV charger installation?"

Avoid quoting exact prices unless you're happy to stand by them. Explain what affects the price instead.

## Step 7: Get listed in the right places

- Your trade body or licensing register's "find an electrician" page
- Manufacturer installer directories (for EV chargers, solar or smart home brands you're certified for)
- Apple Maps, Bing Places and reputable local directories
- Local suppliers and builders you work with

Keep your business name, address and phone number identical everywhere.

## Electrician SEO mistakes to avoid

- No mention of licences, certification or insurance
- Stock photos of perfect panels instead of your real work
- One page for all services
- Missed calls: a lead that hits voicemail often calls the next electrician
- Paying for leads before your free Business Profile is complete

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does SEO take for an electrician?

Your Business Profile can start bringing calls within weeks of verification and steady reviews. Ranking your website for competitive searches usually takes months. See [how long SEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/).

### Should electricians use Local Services Ads?

They can work well for emergency and high-value jobs because you pay per lead, not per click. They work best on top of a strong profile and reviews. Read [are Google Local Services Ads worth it?](/blog/google-local-services-ads-worth-it/)

### Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

The profile brings most local calls, but a website lets you rank for specific services like EV chargers and rewires, and gives customers the confidence to call. Our broader [SEO for contractors guide](/blog/seo-for-contractors/) covers how the two work together.
