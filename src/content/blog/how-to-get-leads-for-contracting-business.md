The most reliable ways for contractors to get more leads are: **show up on Google Maps and search, collect reviews after every job, build referral relationships with people who see your customers first, and respond to every enquiry fast.** Paid lead sites and ads can fill gaps, but the strongest contracting businesses build lead sources they own.

This guide covers the lead sources that work best for contractors, roughly in order of cost and long-term value, plus the step most contractors overlook: converting more of the leads they already get.

## The lead sources, compared

| Source | Cost | Speed | Lead quality | You own it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Referrals and repeat customers | Low | Ongoing | Highest | Yes |
| Google Business Profile | Free | Weeks | High | Mostly |
| Your website and SEO | Low to medium | Months | High | Yes |

## 1. Win more of the leads you already get

Before chasing more leads, look at what happens to the ones you have. Many contractors lose jobs because of:

- **Slow responses.** Homeowners often contact several contractors. The first to respond professionally has an advantage.
- **Missed calls.** If you're on a roof or under a house, you can't answer. Use a voicemail that promises a callback time, an auto-reply text, or an answering service.
- **No follow-up on quotes.** A friendly check-in a few days after sending a quote wins jobs that would otherwise drift.
- **Vague quotes.** Clear, itemized, professional quotes build confidence.

Fixing these costs nothing and can lift your win rate quickly.

## 2. Make your Google Business Profile work hard

For "near me" searches, the Google Maps results are where many homeowners start. A complete, verified profile with the right category, service areas, photos and plenty of reviews is the single best free lead source for most contractors.

If you work from home or at customers' properties, set it up as a service-area business. Our guide to a [Google Business Profile without a storefront](/blog/google-business-profile-without-address/) covers the settings.

## 3. Build a review engine

Reviews turn searchers into callers. Make asking for a review part of every job's close-out:

1. Final walk-through with the customer
2. Text them your Google review link that day
3. Reply to the review when it arrives

Ask every customer, not just the ones you expect to be happy, and never offer anything in exchange. Both are Google rules.

## 4. Get serious about referrals

Your past customers already trust you. To get more referrals:

- **Ask directly** at the end of a job: "If any of your neighbors need similar work, we'd really appreciate the recommendation."
- **Leave something behind:** a few business cards or a fridge magnet.
- **Put up a yard sign** during the job, with permission. Neighbors notice work happening on their street.
- **Stay in touch:** a seasonal reminder or maintenance tip keeps you top of mind.

## 5. Partner with people who see your customers first

Many homeowners ask someone they already trust for recommendations. Build relationships with:

- **Builders and general contractors** who sub out your trade
- **Real estate agents and property managers** who need reliable contractors before and after sales
- **Suppliers and showrooms** where homeowners choose materials
- **Complementary trades:** a concrete contractor and a landscaper, an electrician and a solar installer, a roofer and a gutter specialist
- **Insurance-related businesses,** for trades involved in repairs

Treat these partners well: turn up when you say you will, keep them updated and send work their way too.

## 6. Build a website that turns visitors into quote requests

Your website should make it obvious what you do and where, show proof, and make contacting you effortless on a phone. That means a page for each service, real project photos, reviews, licenses and insurance, and a short quote form. See [what should be on a contractor website](/blog/contractor-website-must-haves/).

Over time, a good website also brings in leads from Google search. Our guide to [SEO for contractors](/blog/seo-for-contractors/) explains how.

## 7. Use paid leads carefully

Paid sources can be useful, especially when you're starting out or have gaps in your schedule.

- **Google Local Services Ads:** appear above normal search results in many trades and countries, and charge for leads rather than clicks.
- **Google Ads:** you pay per click, so a strong landing page matters.
- **Lead platforms:** Angi, Thumbtack, and country-specific sites like Checkatrade, HomeStars, hipages or Builderscrack.

Track every paid lead: how many you got, how many you quoted, how many you won and what each won job cost you. Cut anything that doesn't pay back.

## 8. Show your work on social media

You don't need to dance on TikTok. Simple, consistent posts work for contractors:

- Before and after photos
- Short videos of a job in progress
- Explaining a common problem and how you fix it
- Your crew and your equipment

Social media rarely brings in a flood of leads by itself, but it builds trust and keeps you in front of past customers and their friends.

## 9. Track where every lead comes from

Ask every new enquiry: "How did you find us?" and write it down. Within a couple of months you'll know which sources bring the best jobs, and which you can stop paying for.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the fastest way for a contractor to get leads?

Paid sources like Google Local Services Ads, Google Ads and lead platforms are the fastest. The fastest free option is usually following up on old quotes and asking past customers for referrals. For steady leads over time, focus on your Google Business Profile and reviews.

### How do I get commercial contracting leads?

Commercial work usually comes through relationships rather than searches: general contractors, property managers, facility managers, architects and developers. Build a simple capability statement, show relevant commercial projects on your website, and reach out directly. Reliability and paperwork (insurance, safety records, certifications) matter a lot.

### Is door knocking still worth it for contractors?

For some trades, especially after storms or when working on a street, a respectful approach to neighbors can win work. Check local rules first, as some areas restrict door-to-door sales. A yard sign and a leave-behind card are lower-pressure alternatives.

### How quickly should I respond to a new lead?

As fast as you reasonably can. Homeowners often contact several contractors, and a quick, professional response gives you an advantage. Even a short message saying when you'll call back helps.

## Contractor lead generation checklist

- [ ] Enquiries answered or called back fast
- [ ] Follow-up sent on every quote
- [ ] Google Business Profile complete, verified, service areas set
- [ ] Review link sent after every job
- [ ] Referral ask and leave-behind cards built into job close-out
- [ ] Relationships with builders, agents, suppliers and other trades
- [ ] Website with service pages, project photos and a quick quote form
- [ ] Paid leads tracked to cost per won job
- [ ] Source recorded for every lead

Contractors who build lead sources they own, like reviews, referrals, partners and search visibility, end up less dependent on paid leads and more in control of which jobs they take.
