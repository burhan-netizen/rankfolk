ChatGPT and similar AI assistants (Google Gemini, Claude, Microsoft Copilot) are most useful to a small business as **a fast first-draft writer and a patient sounding board**. They're good at turning your rough notes into a review reply, a social post, an email or a service page. They're bad at knowing your business, your prices or your area, so you always supply the facts and check the result.

This guide shows the jobs where AI genuinely saves time, with prompts you can copy, plus the mistakes to avoid.

## The golden rule: give it your facts

AI tools write generic text when you give them generic instructions. The fix is to tell them about your business first. Save a short "business brief" and paste it at the start of every chat:

```
About my business:
- Name: Northside Decks
- What we do: build and repair timber and composite decks
- Where: north Denver and nearby suburbs
- Customers: homeowners, mostly 35 to 65
- What makes us different: 10-year workmanship guarantee, we send photos daily during the build
- Tone: friendly, plain English, no hype, no exclamation marks
- Never: invent prices, discounts or claims I haven't given you
```

Many AI tools also let you save instructions like this permanently, so you don't need to paste them each time.

## 1. Reply to Google reviews

Replying to every review matters, and it's easy to put off. AI makes it a two-minute job.

```
Using my business brief, write a short reply to this Google review.
Thank them by first name, mention the specific job they described,
and keep it under 60 words. Review: "[paste review]"
```

For a negative review, add: *"Stay calm and polite, don't argue, acknowledge the problem, and invite them to call me on [number] to sort it out."* Always read it before posting. Our guide to [responding to negative Google reviews](/blog/how-to-respond-to-negative-google-reviews/) explains what a good reply needs.

## 2. Write social media posts from a job photo

```
I just finished this job: [describe it in a sentence or two, e.g.
"replaced a rotten deck with composite, 4m x 5m, built-in bench"].
Write 3 short Facebook posts about it, each with a different angle:
the problem we solved, the result, and a tip for homeowners.
No hashtag spam, max 3 hashtags.
```

Pick the best one and add your real photo. One genuine post a week beats seven generic ones.

## 3. Draft website pages

AI can turn your notes into a first draft of a service page, which is often the hardest part to start.

```
Write a service page for [service] in [area] using my business brief.
Structure: a headline that says what and where, 2 short paragraphs on
the problem we solve, what's included (bullets), how the job works
(steps), 4 FAQs customers actually ask, and a call to action.
Leave [BRACKETS] anywhere you need a fact from me.
```

The `[BRACKETS]` instruction is important: it stops the tool making up details. Our guide to [writing website copy with AI](/blog/how-to-write-website-copy-with-ai/) covers this in depth.

## 4. Write Google Business Profile posts and descriptions

```
Write a Google Business Profile description (under 750 characters)
using my business brief. Mention our main services and areas
naturally. No phone numbers, links or ALL CAPS.
```

Google's limit for the business description is 750 characters, and its [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) don't allow links or promotional gimmicks in that field, so check the result.

## 5. Emails and follow-ups

AI is great for the emails you keep meaning to send:

- A follow-up to a quote that went quiet
- A thank-you after a job, with a request for a review
- A polite chase for an overdue invoice
- A seasonal reminder to past customers ("time to service your boiler")

```
Write a short, friendly follow-up email to a customer who received a
quote for [job] 10 days ago and hasn't replied. Don't be pushy. Offer
to answer questions and mention the quote is valid until [date].
```

Need a review request? Our [review request generator](/tools/review-request-generator/) creates ready-made messages that follow Google's rules.

## 6. Brainstorm, plan and research

- **Content ideas:** "List 20 questions homeowners ask before hiring a [trade]." Each good one can become a blog post or FAQ.
- **Ad copy variations:** "Write 10 headlines under 30 characters for a Google ad about [service] in [city]."
- **Customer personas:** "Describe 3 types of customer who hire a [trade], and what each cares about most."
- **Planning:** "Turn this list of marketing tasks into a simple weekly routine I can do in 2 hours."

Treat these as starting points. Your own experience of what customers ask is more valuable than any list.

## What not to use AI for

- **Facts, prices and laws.** AI tools can state wrong information confidently. Check anything factual with an official source.
- **Fake reviews or testimonials.** Writing reviews for yourself breaks Google's policies and consumer protection laws in many countries.
- **Publishing in bulk without editing.** Mass-produced, unedited pages can fall under Google's spam policies. See [is AI content bad for SEO?](/blog/is-ai-content-bad-for-seo/)
- **Customer data.** Don't paste customers' personal details into AI tools unless you're sure how the tool stores and uses them. Check the tool's privacy settings.

## A weekly AI marketing routine (1 hour)

1. **Monday (15 min):** reply to the week's reviews with AI drafts.
2. **Tuesday (15 min):** turn one recent job into a social post and a Google Business Profile post.
3. **Wednesday (15 min):** send follow-ups to quotes and a review request to recent customers.
4. **Friday (15 min):** improve one website page: add FAQs, update photos or rewrite the intro.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ChatGPT free for small businesses?

ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot all offer free versions that are enough for the tasks in this guide. Paid plans add more usage and features; check each provider's pricing page for current details.

### Will customers know my posts were written by AI?

They will if you publish the first draft. Edit it: add your real details, your way of speaking and specifics about the job. That's also what makes it work better.

### Can AI help my business show up in ChatGPT's recommendations?

Indirectly. AI assistants recommend businesses based on what they can find about you online. Our guide to [getting recommended by ChatGPT](/blog/how-to-get-your-business-recommended-by-chatgpt/) explains what helps.

## Summary

- Give AI your business facts first, and tell it never to invent details
- Best uses: review replies, social posts, first drafts of web pages, emails and brainstorming
- Always edit before publishing, and check anything factual
- Never use it for fake reviews or bulk, unedited content
