The most effective way to get more Google reviews is simple: **ask every customer, at the right moment, with a direct link that takes them straight to the review box.** Most happy customers are willing to leave a review. They just don't think of it, or don't know where to go.

This guide shows you how to get your Google review link and QR code, when and how to ask, word-for-word templates you can copy, and the rules you must follow so your reviews don't get removed or your profile restricted.

## Why Google reviews matter so much

- **Ranking:** Google lists review count and rating as part of "prominence", one of the three things that decide local rankings ([Google's local ranking guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).
- **Choice:** reviews are often the first thing customers compare between businesses in the map results.
- **Trust:** detailed, recent reviews answer the question "can I trust these people?" before the customer calls.

## Step 1: Get your Google review link and QR code

Google lets you create a direct review link and a QR code from your Business Profile. According to [Google's instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815), on a computer:

1. Go to your Business Profile (business.google.com).
2. Select **Read reviews**, then **Get more reviews**.
3. Copy the link, or save the QR code image.

Note: Google says QR codes can't be generated on mobile, so use a computer for that step. Save the link somewhere you can grab it quickly, such as a note on your phone or a text shortcut.

## Step 2: Ask at the happiest moment

Timing matters more than wording. Ask when the customer has just experienced the result:

| Business | Best moment to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Contractor | During the final walk-through, then text the link that evening |
| Restaurant or café | When paying, with a QR code on the table or receipt |
| Salon or clinic | At checkout, or in a follow-up text the same day |
| Online or appointment-based service | In the thank-you email right after delivery |

The longer you wait, the less likely people are to do it.

## Step 3: Make it personal and easy

A short, personal request works better than a long generic one. Here are templates you can adapt.

### Text message template

> Hi Sarah, thanks again for choosing us for your new deck. If you have a minute, a quick Google review really helps a small business like ours: [link]. Thanks! Mike, Northside Decks

### Email template

> Subject: Thanks from [Business Name]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for choosing us for [job or service]. It was great working with you.
>
> If you have a moment, we'd really appreciate a review on Google. It helps other people in [area] find us, and we read every one.
>
> [Leave a review button or link]
>
> Thanks again,
> [Your name]

### In person

> "We're a small local business and Google reviews make a huge difference for us. Would you mind leaving one? I'll text you the link so it only takes a minute."

Notice what these don't do: they don't ask for five stars, they don't suggest what to write, and they don't offer anything in return.

## Step 4: Put the QR code where customers are

- Counter or reception desk
- Receipts and invoices
- Business cards and leave-behind cards for contractors
- The back of a quote or job completion sheet
- Table cards in restaurants and cafés
- Your email signature (as a link)

## Step 5: Reply to every review

Google lists replying to reviews as one of its tips for getting more of them ([Google's review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7035772)). Replies show future customers that you're listening, and they encourage others to leave feedback.

- **Positive reviews:** thank them by name and mention the job if you can.
- **Negative reviews:** stay calm, apologize where it's fair, and offer to sort it out offline.

We have full examples in our guide on [how to respond to negative Google reviews](/blog/how-to-respond-to-negative-google-reviews/).

## The rules you must follow

Google's [prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) is strict about reviews. Breaking it can get reviews removed, stop new reviews being posted for a period, or show a warning on your profile.

**You must not:**

- Offer **discounts, free items, payments or other incentives** in exchange for reviews
- **Selectively ask** only customers you think will leave positive reviews ("review gating")
- **Discourage** negative reviews
- **Pressure customers** to leave a review while on your premises
- Set **staff quotas** or ask for reviews that name specific staff members
- Write reviews of your own business, or have staff, friends or family do it
- Offer anything to get a negative review **changed or removed**
- Post reviews on **competitors'** listings

**You can:** ask every customer for an honest review, make it easy with a link or QR code, and reply to all reviews.

## Common reasons reviews don't appear

- **Spam filters:** Google uses automated spam detection, and genuine reviews are sometimes caught by it.
- **The customer doesn't have a Google Account.** Google notes that customers need one, but it can use a non-Gmail email address.
- **Policy issues** in the review text, such as links or offensive language.

If a genuine review disappears, there's usually not much you can do except keep collecting new ones steadily.

## Build a review habit

The businesses with hundreds of reviews didn't get them in a campaign. They asked every customer, every time. Some ways to make it automatic:

- Add "send review link" to your job completion checklist
- Set up an automatic follow-up email or text in your booking or invoicing software
- Track how many requests you send each week, not just reviews received

## Frequently asked questions

### How many Google reviews do I need?

There's no magic number. What matters is how you compare with the businesses around you and whether your reviews are recent. Aim to keep a steady flow coming in every month rather than chasing a single target.

### Can I ask friends and family for reviews?

Only if they were genuine customers who actually used your service. Google's policies treat reviews from people with a conflict of interest, such as close relations, employees or business partners, as against the rules.

### Is it OK to text customers a review link?

Yes. Sending your review link by text, email or WhatsApp is one of the easiest ways to ask, and Google itself suggests sharing the link with customers. Just send it to every customer, not only the ones you expect to be happy.
