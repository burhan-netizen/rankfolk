**SEO for contractors** means getting your business to show up when homeowners and property managers in your area search Google for your trade: "concrete contractor near me", "deck builder in Calgary", "emergency electrician Brisbane". Done well, it brings in calls you don't pay for per lead.

Most contractors don't need a big agency or complicated tactics. You need three things working together: a strong Google Business Profile, a website with a page for each service you offer, and a steady flow of reviews. This guide walks through each one, specifically for contractors.

## Why SEO works so well for contractors

Think about how people hire you. They don't scroll Instagram looking for a roofer for fun. They search when they have a problem or a project: a leak, a cracked driveway, a kitchen they hate. That means the person searching is often ready to hire **now**.

Compared with paid lead sites, where the same lead is often sold to several contractors, someone who finds you directly on Google is calling you and only you. That's why ranking well is worth the effort.

## How homeowners search for contractors

Contractor searches tend to fall into three groups. Your SEO should cover all three:

| Type of search | Example | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trade + near me | "fencing contractor near me" | A strong Google Business Profile |
| Service + location | "stamped concrete patio Denver" | A dedicated service page |
| Problem or question | "how much does a new roof cost" | A helpful article or FAQ |

## Step 1: Set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business

Most contractors work at the customer's property, not from a shopfront. Google calls this a **service-area business**. If customers don't visit your address, Google requires you to hide it and show the areas you serve instead ([Google's service-area rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137)).

Key settings for contractors:

- **Primary category:** be specific. "Concrete contractor", "Roofing contractor", "Electrician", "Fence contractor", rather than "General contractor" unless that's truly what you are.
- **Service areas:** up to 20 areas. Google recommends staying within about two hours' drive of your base.
- **Services:** add every service you offer, with a short description.
- **Photos:** before and after shots, your crew, your branded vehicles and equipment.

We have a full guide on [setting up a Google Business Profile without a storefront](/blog/google-business-profile-without-address/) if you work from home.

## Step 2: Build a website that matches how people search

A one-page website with your logo, a phone number and "we do it all" won't rank for much. Google needs pages to match against searches.

Build a page for each main service. A concrete contractor might have separate pages for:

- Driveways
- Patios and stamped concrete
- Foundations and slabs
- Concrete repair
- Commercial concrete

Each service page should include:

- A heading with the service and your main area ("Concrete Driveways in Fort Worth")
- What the job involves, from quote to finish
- Photos of real projects (your own, not stock photos)
- What affects the price, even if you don't list numbers
- Reviews from customers who had that service
- A clear "Call for a free quote" button and a short form

Not sure what else belongs on the site? See our list of [what should be on a contractor website](/blog/contractor-website-must-haves/).

## Step 3: Show your work, because it's your best SEO content

Contractors have something most businesses don't: visible, photographable results. Use them.

- Add a **projects** or **gallery** section, organized by service.
- Write a short paragraph for each project: where it was, what the problem was, what you did.
- Add the same photos to your Google Business Profile.

This gives Google fresh, relevant content, and it gives homeowners proof you've done jobs like theirs.

## Step 4: Get reviews after every job

Reviews matter for ranking, and they matter even more for winning the job once someone sees you. For contractors, the best time to ask is **the moment the customer sees the finished work** and is happiest.

A simple routine:

1. Finish the job and walk the customer through it.
2. Text them your Google review link that evening.
3. Reply to the review when it comes in.

Ask every customer, not just the ones you expect to be happy. Google's policies don't allow selectively asking for positive reviews or offering anything in return ([Google's review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). For message templates, see [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## Step 5: Get listed where contractors are trusted

Links and listings from trusted sites help prominence. Good options for contractors:

- **Manufacturer and supplier "find an installer" pages.** If you're certified with a roofing, flooring or HVAC brand, ask to be listed.
- **Trade associations and licensing bodies** in your state, province or country.
- **Local business associations.**
- **Home service directories** relevant to your country (Houzz and Yelp in the US, Checkatrade or TrustATrader in the UK, HomeStars in Canada, hipages in Australia, Builderscrack in New Zealand).
- **Builders, designers and real estate agents** you work with regularly. A "recommended contractors" link from them is valuable.

Keep your business name, phone number and address identical everywhere.

## Step 6: Answer the questions homeowners ask

Homeowners research before they call. They search things like "how long does a concrete driveway last", "do I need a permit for a fence" or "roof repair vs replacement". If your website answers those questions clearly, you show up earlier in their decision and build trust before the first call.

You don't need to blog every week. A handful of genuinely useful articles or a solid FAQ page, based on questions customers actually ask you on site, is plenty to start.

## Step 7: Make it easy to contact you on a phone

Most contractor searches happen on phones, often in a hurry. Check your website on your own phone:

- Is the phone number visible and tappable at the top of every page?
- Does the page load quickly on mobile data?
- Is the quote form short (name, phone, address, job description)?
- Can someone send photos of the job?

If calling you takes more than one tap, you're losing jobs to competitors.

## What about paid ads and lead sites?

SEO takes time. While it builds, many contractors also use Google Local Services Ads, Google Ads or lead platforms. That's fine, as long as you're building something you own at the same time. Paid leads stop the day you stop paying; your rankings and reviews keep working.

## Common contractor SEO mistakes

- **Using stock photos** instead of your own project photos
- **One "Services" page** listing everything in bullet points
- **Adding city names to your Google business name** to rank in more places (it can get you suspended)
- **Using a virtual office** to appear in another city
- **Never asking for reviews** because "customers will leave them if they're happy" (most won't unless asked)
- **Hiring anyone who guarantees #1 rankings.** Google says no one can guarantee that

## Contractor SEO checklist

- [ ] Google Business Profile set up as a service-area business, verified, specific category
- [ ] All services listed on your Google profile
- [ ] Website with a separate page for each main service
- [ ] Project photos on your website and Google profile
- [ ] Review request sent after every job
- [ ] Every review replied to
- [ ] Listed with manufacturers, associations and trusted directories
- [ ] Phone number tappable at the top of every page
- [ ] A few helpful answers to common customer questions

Start with your Google profile and reviews this week. Then build out one service page at a time.
