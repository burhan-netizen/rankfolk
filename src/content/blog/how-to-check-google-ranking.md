The most accurate free way to check where your website ranks on Google is **Google Search Console**. Its Performance report shows the searches your site appears for and your **average position** for each, based on real searches. Searching for yourself on your own phone is the least reliable way, because Google personalizes and localizes results.

This guide explains the free methods that give you a true picture, how to check your Google Maps ranking, and which numbers matter more than rankings.

## Why searching for yourself is misleading

When you search for your own business on your own phone or computer, results can be affected by:

- **Your location:** local results change from street to street
- **Your search history:** Google may show sites you visit often higher
- **Your device:** mobile and desktop results can differ
- **Timing:** results move around day to day

So "I'm third when I search" may not be what your customers see, especially if they're on the other side of town.

## Method 1: Google Search Console (most accurate)

Search Console reports on real searches by real people. If you haven't set it up, follow our [Google Search Console setup guide](/blog/how-to-set-up-google-search-console/).

To check your rankings:

1. Open **Performance** (Search results).
2. Make sure **Average position** is ticked at the top.
3. Select the **Queries** tab to see the searches you appear for.
4. Select the **Pages** tab to see positions for each page.
5. Change the date range to compare this month with last month.

### How to read average position

- **1 to 3:** at or near the top of page one
- **4 to 10:** on page one, lower down
- **11 to 20:** page two, the best opportunities to improve
- **20+:** further back

Average position is just that, an average. A position of 8 might mean you're sometimes 3rd and sometimes 15th, depending on where and how people searched.

### Filter for your most important searches

Use **+ Add filter**, then **Query**, and type a word like your main service. This shows just the searches containing that word, so you can track how they change over time.

## Method 2: A clean manual search

For a quick spot check, reduce personalization:

1. Open a **private or incognito window**.
2. Search for a realistic phrase customers would use, including the location, for example "kitchen renovations Edinburgh".
3. Note where your site appears.

This still won't be perfect, especially for local searches, but it's closer to what a new customer sees than a search on your usual browser.

## Method 3: Check your Google Maps ranking

Map results depend heavily on where the searcher is. Google ranks local results by relevance, distance and prominence ([source](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)), so you'll naturally rank higher near your base.

Free ways to get a picture:

- **Search from different places.** When you or your team are on jobs around town, search your main service and note your position.
- **Ask a few customers or friends** in different areas to tell you what they see.
- **Check your Google Business Profile performance:** it shows the searches people used to find your profile, plus calls, direction requests and website clicks. If those rise, your visibility is improving.

Paid "local rank grid" tools can map your ranking across an area automatically. They're useful but optional.

## Method 4: Free versions of SEO tools

Several SEO platforms offer free rank checks or free tiers with limited searches. They estimate positions using their own data, so treat them as a guide. For most small businesses, Search Console is more trustworthy because it's based on your actual search data from Google.

## What to track instead of (just) rankings

Rankings are a means to an end. Track these monthly:

| Metric | Where to find it | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website clicks from Google | Search Console | Real visitors from search |
| Impressions | Search Console | How visible you are, even before clicks grow |
| Calls and direction requests | Google Business Profile | Real customer actions from Maps |
| Enquiries and bookings | Your inbox, forms, phone | What actually pays the bills |
| How new customers found you | Ask them | Confirms what's working |

If clicks, calls and enquiries are rising, your SEO is working, even if one particular ranking bounces around.

## What to do with your ranking data

- **Page two searches (positions 11 to 20):** improve those pages first. Add detail, answer more questions, improve the title, add photos.
- **High impressions, low clicks:** rewrite the page title and description so they're clearer and more appealing.
- **Searches you don't appear for at all:** you may need a new page for that service.
- **Rankings dropped suddenly:** check Search Console for indexing problems or messages, and check whether anything changed on your site.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I check my Google ranking for free?

Use Google Search Console. Open the Performance report, turn on Average position, and look at the Queries tab. It's free, it's based on real searches, and it covers every search your site appears for, not just the ones you think to check.

### Why is my average position different from what I see when I search?

Average position combines every time your site appeared, across many locations, devices and search variations. Your own search is one result, from one place, influenced by your history. Both can be "true", but Search Console's average is a better guide to what customers see overall.

### How often do Google rankings change?

Constantly. Small movements from day to day are normal. Look at trends over weeks and months, and compare the same date ranges, rather than reacting to daily changes.

### Can I see my competitors' rankings for free?

Not in Search Console, which only shows your own site. You can search manually in a private window to see who ranks for key phrases, or use the limited free versions of paid SEO tools for estimates.

### What's a good ranking for a small business?

For local searches, being in the map results and on page one for your main services in your main area is a strong position. But the real measure is whether Google brings you enough calls and enquiries. A business ranking fifth with lots of great reviews can win more customers than one ranking first with poor reviews.

### Do Google rankings show up in Google Analytics?

Not directly. Analytics shows visits from Google, but not your position for each search. You can link Search Console to Analytics to see some search data in one place, but Search Console remains the main source for positions.

## Summary

- Search Console is the most accurate free way to check rankings.
- Searching for yourself on your own device is misleading.
- Map rankings change with the searcher's location; check from different places and watch your profile insights.
- Track clicks, calls and enquiries, not just positions.
- Focus improvements on page-two searches first.
